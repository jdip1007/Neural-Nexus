    A shared neural basis underlying psychiatric comorbidity¶

    Source¶

    Field Value
    Type article
    URL https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02317-4
    Author Chao Xie, Shitong Xiang, Chun Shen, Xuerui Peng, et al.
    Published Nature Medicine, Volume 29, May 2023
    Ingested 2025-01-21

    TL;DR¶

    Researchers identified a neuropsychopathological (NP) factor — a reproducible neural signature that underlies both externalizing (ADHD, ASD, ODD, CD) and internalizing (depression, GAD, ED, SP) symptoms. This NP factor represents delayed development of prefrontal circuits, leading to poor executive function, and generalizes across development, imaging modalities, and clinical populations.

    Key Points¶

    1. NP Factor Identification: Using multitask fMRI connectomes from 1,750 adolescents (IMAGEN cohort), researchers extracted shared neural edges across 8 psychiatric symptoms (4 externalizing, 4 internalizing), revealing a crossdisorder neural factor.

    2. Neural Circuits: The NP factor primarily involves three networks — sensorimotor network (SMF), salience network (SAL), and frontoparietal network (FPN) — with key regions including ventral precuneus, medial prefrontal cortex, and inferior frontal gyrus.

    3. Neurobehavioral Correlates: Higher NP factor scores (indicating weaker connectivity) are associated with poor executive function — lower IQ, reduced working memory, and impaired risk adjustment — but not with non-executive cognitive domains.

    4. Genetic Basis: The NP factor is genetically associated with polygenic risk scores for ADHD and depression, and gene ontology analysis shows enrichment for neurodevelopmental processes (neuronal differentiation, axon guidance, synapse formation).

    5. Developmental Trajectory: NP factor scores decrease with age, indicating normalization during development. Higher scores at age 14 predict slower symptom reduction from adolescence to young adulthood.

    6. Generalizability: The NP factor is reproducible across multiple developmental periods (ages 10-29), generalizable to resting-state connectomes, and validates in clinical samples (ADHD-200, STRATIFY/ESTRA covering AN, BN, AUD, MDD).

    Notable Quotes¶

    "We demonstrate that this NP factor might represent a unified, genetically determined, delayed development of the prefrontal cortex that further leads to poor executive function."

    "The NP factor primarily involved three networks: SMF, SAL and FPN. These networks are critical for executive function, cognitive control and salience processing."

    "Adolescence is a critical period for the maturation of these networks and that delayed development of prefrontal circuits may contribute to psychopathology."

    Detailed Notes¶

    Background and Problem¶

    Psychiatric comorbidity — coexistence of multiple psychiatric conditions — affects 27.9% of adolescents aged 14-17. Individuals with comorbid diagnoses experience poorer outcomes and severe cognitive deficits. Traditional approaches treated comorbidity as a confounding factor to be removed, but recent evidence suggests shared neurobiological origins across disorders.

    The NP Factor¶

    Using connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM) on multitask fMRI (stop-signal task for response inhibition, monetary incentive delay for reward processing, emotional face task for emotion reactivity), researchers built symptom-specific brain signatures for 8 disorders. Shared edges across all symptoms formed the NP factor.

    Reliability: Test-retest reliability across 11 training-validation folds was ICC = 0.94 (excellent).

    Longitudinal Stability: NP factor scores at age 19 correlated with scores at age 14 (r = 0.57), demonstrating temporal stability.

    Neural Architecture¶

    Positive edges (associated with higher symptoms): Weaker FC within SMF, SAL, and FPN networks. Reduced connectivity correlates with symptom severity.

    Negative edges (associated with lower symptoms): Stronger FC between these networks and other brain regions. Enhanced connectivity is protective.

    Key regions: Ventral precuneus (self-referential processing), medial prefrontal cortex (emotion regulation, decision-making), inferior frontal gyrus (response inhibition, cognitive control).

    Processing speed NS NS

    Conclusion: The NP factor is specifically associated with executive function deficits, not general cognitive impairment.

    Genetic Characterization¶

    • ADHD PRS: r = -0.10, P = 0.002
    • Depression PRS: r = -0.09, P = 0.004
    • Gene ontology: Enrichment for neurodevelopmental processes (P < 0.001)
    • No enrichment for immune or metabolic pathways

    Generalizability¶

    Developmental: Predicts symptoms across ages 10-29 in ABCD, IMAGEN, and HCP cohorts (r = 0.15-0.35, P < 0.01).

    Modality: Resting-state NP factor predicts externalizing (r = 0.22) and internalizing (r = 0.18) symptoms.

    Clinical samples: - ADHD-200 (N = 520): Predicts ADHD symptom severity (r = 0.28) - STRATIFY/ESTRA (N = 413): Predicts symptoms across AN, BN, AUD, MDD (r = 0.19-0.26)

    Limitations¶

    1. IMAGEN cohort is primarily European ancestry (population-based, limited diversity)
    2. Clinical samples had smaller sizes and different age ranges
    3. Limited cognitive assessment (executive function, processing speed, memory only)
    4. Environmental factors not examined (genetic-only focus)

    My Takeaways¶

    This study bridges internalizing-externalizing divide by identifying shared neural circuits. The NP factor represents a mechanistic link between genetic risk, brain development, and cognitive dysfunction. Most importantly, adolescence emerges as a critical window — connectivity patterns normalize during development, but delayed maturation predicts chronic symptoms. This suggests early intervention targeting prefrontal circuits during adolescence could prevent chronic psychopathology.

    The methodological strength is impressive: 11-fold cross-validation for reliability, longitudinal testing for stability, and validation across 3 cohorts, 2 modalities, and 2 clinical samples. The limitation is the homogeneity of the IMAGEN cohort (European ancestry), limiting generalizability to other populations.

    The NP factor's specificity to executive function (not all cognition) is clinically meaningful: it suggests interventions should target executive control rather than broad cognitive enhancement.
