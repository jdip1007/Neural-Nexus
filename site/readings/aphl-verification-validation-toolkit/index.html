    APHL Verification and Validation Toolkit¶

    Complete comprehensive toolkit for clinical laboratory verification and validation studies, published by the Association of Public Health Laboratories (APHL) to assist laboratories in meeting CLIA regulatory requirements.

    Document Overview¶

    Title: Laboratory Test Verification and Validation Toolkit Publisher: Association of Public Health Laboratories (APHL) Publication Date: March 2024 Pages: 37 (complete toolkit) Sections: 8 main sections + reference materials

    Purpose and Scope¶

    The toolkit helps clinical laboratories: 1. Determine the difference between verification and validation 2. Understand when each process should be performed 3. Plan, implement, and analyze verification/validation studies 4. Meet CLIA regulatory requirements 5. Ensure quality of test results before patient testing

    Document Structure¶

    Section 1: Verification and Validation 101¶

    Content: Core concepts, decision flow, required performance characteristics

    Key Topics: - Definition of verification vs validation - Decision tree for determining V&V requirements - Required performance characteristics for qualitative and quantitative assays - General V&V plan steps (5 phases) - Regulatory requirements overview

    Section 2: Checklist for V&V Process¶

    Content: Comprehensive 5-phase checklist covering entire V&V project

    Phases: 1. Plan Development (8 items) 2. Initiation (5 items) 3. Testing (7 items) 4. Summary Report (5 items) 5. Implementation (5 items)

    Section 3: Obtaining Appropriate Test Samples¶

    Content: Sample types, numbers, and volume requirements

    Sample Types: - Patient specimens (preferred for verification) - Reference materials - Proficiency testing samples - Pooled specimens

    Sample Numbers: - Precision: Minimum 20 replicates - Accuracy: Minimum 40 specimens - LoD: Minimum 20 replicates at low concentration - Reference intervals: 20 (verify) or 120 (establish)

    Volume Requirements: - Minimum 2-3 mL per specimen - Additional volume for repeat testing - Account for aliquot needs

    Section 4: Qualitative Assays¶

    Content: Performance characteristics for qualitative (yes/no) tests

    Characteristics Covered: 1. Accuracy (Method Comparison) - Percent agreement ≥ 95% - Kappa statistic ≥ 0.75 - Sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV

    1. Precision (Repeatability)
    2. Within-run agreement ≥ 95%

    3. Between-run agreement ≥ 90%

    4. Analytical Sensitivity (LoD)

    5. Probit analysis method

    6. ≥ 19/20 (95%) detection at LoD

    7. Analytical Specificity

    8. Cross-reactivity testing
    9. Interference testing

    10. No false positives from unrelated substances

    11. Reportable Range

    12. Confirm manufacturer's stated range

    13. Test low, mid, high concentrations

    14. Reference Range

    15. Verify with 20 reference individuals
    16. Confirm ≥ 90% within published range

    Section 5: Quantitative Assays¶

    Content: Performance characteristics for quantitative (numerical) tests

    Additional Characteristics: - Calibration verification (every 6 months) - Calibration curve evaluation (r ≥ 0.99) - Limit of Quantitation (LoQ) - Weighted regression (if needed)

    Acceptance Criteria: - Bias within ±10% - CV: ≤ 5% (within-run), ≤ 7.5% (between-run), ≤ 10% (total) - LoD: 95% detection probability - LoQ: CV ≤ 20%

    Statistical Methods: - Paired t-test, Deming regression - Bland-Altman plot - Passing-Bablok regression - ANOVA for precision - Probit analysis for LoD

    Section 6: Related Processes¶

    Content: Ongoing quality management activities

    Topics: 1. Sample/Analyte Stability - Room temp, refrigerated, frozen stability - Freeze-thaw cycles - Acceptance: bias within ±10%

    1. Bridging Studies
    2. Modifications requiring new validation
    3. Sample comparison before/after change

    4. Acceptance: bias within ±10%, r ≥ 0.95

    5. Instrumentation V&V

    6. Installation Qualification (IQ)
    7. Operational Qualification (OQ)

    8. Performance Qualification (PQ)

    9. Reagent Lot-to-Lot Comparison

    10. Test 20-40 samples with both lots

    11. Acceptance: bias within ±10%

    12. Software Upgrades

    13. Impact assessment required
    14. Verification after major upgrades

    Section 7: Safety Considerations and Risk Assessments¶

    Content: Laboratory safety and risk management

    Risk Assessment Process: 1. Hazard identification 2. Risk analysis (likelihood × severity) 3. Risk evaluation against criteria 4. Risk mitigation (engineering, administrative, PPE) 5. Monitoring and review (at least annually)

    Risk Categories: - Biological risks (infectious agents, biosafety level) - Chemical risks (hazardous reagents, SDS) - Physical risks (electrical, mechanical, thermal) - Ergonomic risks (repetitive motion, heavy lifting)

    Best Practices Summary¶

    Before V&V¶

    1. Clearly define test method and intended use
    2. Determine verification vs validation
    3. Select team and assign responsibilities
    4. Establish timeline and budget
    5. Obtain necessary approvals

    During V&V¶

    1. Follow protocol strictly
    2. Document all activities
    3. Track deviations
    4. Perform statistical analysis properly
    5. Compare to acceptance criteria

    After V&V¶

    1. Prepare comprehensive report
    2. Obtain final approvals
    3. Update procedures
    4. Train staff
    5. Implement QC program

    Common Pitfalls¶

    • Insufficient sample size
    • Inadequate sample range
    • Wrong statistical methods
    • Not following protocol
    • Incomplete documentation
    • Missing acceptance criteria
    • No ongoing QC plan
