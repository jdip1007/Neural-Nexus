    預設醫療指示與不作心肺復甦命令¶

    Overview¶

    This concept covers the legal framework for advance medical directives in Hong Kong, focusing on advance medical directives (預設醫療指示) and do not attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation orders (不作心肺復甦命令). These legal instruments allow individuals to specify their medical treatment preferences when they lose decision-making capacity, protecting patient autonomy and preventing family conflicts during medical emergencies.

    Key Components¶

    Advance Medical Directives (預設醫療指示)¶

    Advance medical directives are legal documents that allow competent adults to specify their wishes regarding life-sustaining treatment when they lose capacity.

    Legal Requirements: - Must be 18+ years old with mental capacity - Must understand the consequences of refusing treatment - Requires two witnesses, one being a registered doctor - Must be written when the person has decision-making capacity

    Scope: - Specifies preferences for life-sustaining treatment - Applies when person loses mental capacity - Cannot include requests for euthanasia or assisted death - Ensures natural death process with basic care and palliative treatment

    Do Not Attempt Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation Orders (不作心肺復甦命令)¶

    DNACPR orders are medical orders issued by doctors when resuscitation would be harmful or against the patient's best interests.

    Medical Process: - Issued by two registered doctors jointly - Based on professional medical judgment - Applied when resuscitation would be harmful or futile - Cannot be issued against the patient's previously expressed wishes

    For Incapacitated Patients: - Doctors must consult with responsible persons (family, guardians) - If no responsible person found, doctors consider patient's past wishes and values - Design protects medical decision-making professionalism while respecting family rights

    Legal Framework¶

    Hong Kong Legal Basis¶

    The legal framework is established under the Medical Treatment (End of Life) Ordinance, which provides:

    1. Patient Autonomy: Right to make decisions about one's own medical care
    2. Medical Decision-Making: Clear processes for incapacitated patients
    3. Family Protection: Prevents families from feeling decisions were made secretly
    4. Professional Standards: Ensures medical decisions follow professional guidelines

    Key Legal Distinctions¶

    Advance Directives vs Euthanasia: - Advance directives allow refusal of treatment but not active killing - Cannot request "please give me euthanasia" or "don't give me water" - Focuses on allowing natural death process with appropriate care - Euthanasia and medical assistance in dying remain illegal in Hong Kong

    Medical Decision-Making Process: - For competent patients: Direct personal choice via advance directives - For incompetent patients: Doctor consultation with responsible persons - Fallback mechanism: Based on patient's previously expressed wishes and values

    Practical Applications¶

    Emergency Situations¶

    When patients suddenly lose consciousness (e.g., from accidents, medical emergencies):

    1. Without Planning: Families face difficult decisions, potential conflicts
    2. With Advance Directives: Clear guidance reduces family stress and suffering
    3. Medical Response: Doctors follow documented wishes rather than defaulting to aggressive treatment

    Patient Scenarios¶

    Terminal Cancer Patients: - May specify preferences for comfort over aggressive treatment - Can avoid painful resuscitation procedures - Ensure dignified death process

    Elderly Patients: - Prevent unnecessary suffering from aggressive interventions - Allow natural death with appropriate palliative care - Reduce family burden during difficult times

    Emergency Situations: - Sudden loss of consciousness from accidents - Medical emergencies where quick decisions are needed - Prevents "default" aggressive treatment that may cause harm

    Ethical Considerations¶

    Patient Rights¶

    • Autonomy: Right to control medical treatment decisions
    • Dignity: Right to die with dignity without unnecessary suffering
    • Informed Consent: Must understand consequences of treatment decisions
    • Privacy: Medical decisions remain confidential until needed

    Family Considerations¶

    • Reduced Burden: Families don't have to make difficult decisions under stress
    • Conflict Prevention: Prevents family disputes about treatment preferences
    • Emotional Protection: Reduces guilt and stress during medical emergencies

    Medical Professional Responsibilities¶

    • Professional Judgment: Must act in patient's best interests
    • Communication: Must explain rationale clearly to families
    • Documentation: Proper procedures and witness requirements
    • Ethical Practice: Balance patient wishes with medical ethics

    Implementation Process¶

    Creating Advance Medical Directives¶

    1. Assessment: Confirm mental capacity and understanding
    2. Discussion: Explain treatment options and consequences
    3. Documentation: Write clear, specific preferences
    4. Witnessing: Two witnesses required (one registered doctor)
    5. Storage: Keep document accessible to medical providers
    6. Review: Periodically review and update as needed

    Medical Order Process for DNACPR¶

    1. Medical Assessment: Determine if resuscitation would be harmful or futile
    2. Professional Consultation: Joint decision by two registered doctors
    3. Family Consultation: Discuss with responsible persons if available
    4. Documentation: Complete proper medical order forms
    5. Implementation: Ensure all medical staff aware of the order
    6. Review: Regularly reassess patient condition

    International Context¶

    While this concept focuses on Hong Kong law, similar advance directive systems exist in many jurisdictions:

    • Common Law Countries: UK, Australia, Canada, Singapore
    • Civil Law Countries: Many European countries with variations
    • Key Differences: Legal requirements, scope of authority, cultural acceptance
