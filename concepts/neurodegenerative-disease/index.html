    Neurodegenerative disease¶

    Neurodegenerative diseases are a heterogeneous group of disorders characterized by progressive loss of structure and function of neurons, including death of neurons. These diseases typically involve protein misfolding and aggregation, synaptic dysfunction, and ultimately neuronal loss in specific brain regions. Neurodegeneration is closely linked to metabolic-disorders, particularly obesity, and involves dysfunction of the neurovascular-unit.

    Common Features¶

    Protein Misfolding and Aggregation¶

    Mechanisms: - Misfolded proteins form insoluble aggregates - Aggregates are toxic to neurons - Impaired protein clearance systems

    Key proteins: - Amyloid-β and tau: Alzheimer's disease - Alpha-synuclein: Parkinson's disease - Huntingtin: Huntington's disease - TDP-43: ALS, frontotemporal dementia

    Neuronal Loss¶

    Progressive nature: - Gradual death of neurons - Selective vulnerability of specific populations - Irreversible damage

    Consequences: - Cognitive decline - Motor dysfunction - Behavioral changes

    Synaptic Dysfunction¶

    Early event: - Synaptic loss precedes neuronal death - Impaired neurotransmission - Disrupted neural networks

    Mechanisms: - Toxic protein effects on synapses - Glial dysfunction - Impaired neurovascular coupling

    Neuroinflammation¶

    Chronic inflammation: - Microglial activation - Astrocyte reactivity - Cytokine release

    Dual role: - Initially protective (clearance of debris) - Becomes detrimental (chronic damage)

    Major Neurodegenerative Diseases¶

    Alzheimer's Disease¶

    Pathology: - Extracellular amyloid-β plaques - Intracellular neurofibrillary tangles (tau) - Widespread cortical and hippocampal atrophy

    Symptoms: - Progressive memory loss - Cognitive decline - Behavioral changes - Loss of independence

    Risk factors: - Age (primary risk factor) - APOE4 genotype - Obesity and metabolic syndrome - Cardiovascular disease

    See: [[alzheimers-disease]] for detailed discussion

    Parkinson's Disease¶

    Pathology: - Loss of dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra - Alpha-synuclein aggregates (Lewy bodies) - Motor circuit dysfunction

    Symptoms: - Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia - Postural instability - Non-motor symptoms (cognitive, autonomic)

    Risk factors: - Age - Environmental toxins - Genetic factors - Possible metabolic risk factors

    See: [[parkinsons-disease]] for detailed discussion

    Vascular Dementia¶

    Pathology: - Cerebrovascular disease - Chronic cerebral hypoperfusion - White matter lesions - Microinfarcts

    Symptoms: - Stepwise cognitive decline - Executive dysfunction - Gait disturbances

    Risk factors: - Hypertension - Diabetes - Obesity - Cardiovascular disease

    Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)¶

    Pathology: - Motor neuron degeneration - TDP-43 protein aggregation - Glial dysfunction

    Symptoms: - Progressive muscle weakness - Atrophy - Paralysis - Respiratory failure

    Huntington's Disease¶

    Pathology: - CAG repeat expansion in huntingtin gene - Mutant huntingtin protein aggregation - Striatal degeneration

    Symptoms: - Chorea (involuntary movements) - Cognitive decline - Psychiatric symptoms

    Genetic: - Autosomal dominant - Age-dependent penetrance

    Role of Metabolic Disorders¶

    Obesity and Neurodegeneration¶

    Epidemiological evidence: - Midlife obesity increases risk of Alzheimer's and Parkinson's - Higher BMI associated with faster cognitive decline - Visceral adiposity particularly harmful

    Mechanisms: 1. Systemic inflammation: Cytokines cross BBB, activate microglia 2. Insulin resistance: Impaired neuronal metabolism 3. Vascular dysfunction: Reduced cerebral blood flow 4. BBB breakdown: Immune cell infiltration 5. Glymphatic impairment: Reduced waste clearance

    Convergent-cascade model: - Obesity creates multiple parallel disruptions - Interactions create emergent vulnerabilities - Regional differences in resilience determine where degeneration manifests

    Diabetes and Cognitive Decline¶

    Type 2 diabetes: - Increased risk of vascular dementia - Accelerated cognitive decline - Worsened Alzheimer's pathology

    Mechanisms: - Hyperglycemia damages blood vessels - Advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) - Insulin resistance in brain - Impaired glucose metabolism

    Neurovascular Unit Dysfunction¶

    BBB Breakdown¶

    Consequences: - Peripheral immune cell infiltration - Neurotoxic substance entry - Disrupted ion homeostasis - Reduced clearance of toxic proteins

    See: blood-brain-barrier

    Impaired Neurovascular Coupling¶

    Consequences: - Energy deficits in active neurons - Metabolic stress - Neuronal dysfunction - Progressive degeneration

    See: neurovascular-coupling

    Glymphatic Dysfunction¶

    Consequences: - Accumulation of protein aggregates - Reduced waste clearance - Impaired nutrient delivery

    See: glymphatic

    Risk Factors¶

    Non-Modifiable¶

    Genetics: - APOE4 (Alzheimer's) - Mutations in familial forms - Polygenic risk scores

    Age: - Primary risk factor for most neurodegenerative diseases - Cumulative damage over time

    Sex: - Some diseases show sex differences - Hormonal influences

    Modifiable¶

    Metabolic: - Obesity - Type 2 diabetes - Metabolic syndrome - Dyslipidemia

    Cardiovascular: - Hypertension - Atherosclerosis - Smoking

    Lifestyle: - Physical inactivity - Poor diet - Sleep disturbances - Chronic stress

    Environmental: - Toxins (pesticides, heavy metals) - Traumatic brain injury

    Biomarkers¶

    Imaging¶

    Structural MRI: - Brain atrophy patterns - Regional volume loss - White matter hyperintensities

    Functional MRI: - Altered connectivity - Hypometabolism patterns - Network dysfunction

    PET imaging: - Amyloid PET (Alzheimer's) - Tau PET (Alzheimer's) - Dopamine transporter PET (Parkinson's) - Glucose metabolism (FDG-PET)

    Fluid Biomarkers¶

    CSF: - Aβ42 (decreased in Alzheimer's) - Phospho-tau (increased in Alzheimer's) - Neurofilament light chain (neuronal damage) - Synaptic proteins

    Blood: - NfL (neurofilament light chain) - GFAP (glial activation) - Inflammatory markers

    Therapeutic Approaches¶

    Disease-Modifying Therapies¶

    Anti-amyloid antibodies: - Aducanumab, lecanemab, donanemab - Target amyloid plaques in Alzheimer's - Show modest clinical benefit

    Anti-tau therapies: - In development - Target tau aggregation and spread

    Gene therapies: - Huntington's disease (gene silencing) - Spinal muscular atrophy (approved) - ALS (in development)

    Symptomatic Treatments¶

    Alzheimer's: - Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine) - NMDA receptor antagonist (memantine)

    Parkinson's: - Levodopa - Dopamine agonists - MAO-B inhibitors

    Neuroprotective Strategies¶

    Metabolic optimization: - Weight loss - Insulin sensitization - Blood pressure control - Lipid management

    Lifestyle interventions: - Exercise - Cognitive stimulation - Sleep optimization - Mediterranean diet

    Vascular protection: - Anti-inflammatory approaches - BBB support - Enhanced glymphatic function

    Research Advances¶

    Single-Cell Technologies¶

    Cell-type-specific responses: - Understanding selective vulnerability - Identifying new therapeutic targets - Characterizing disease progression

    Genetic Therapies¶

    Gene editing: - CRISPR/Cas9 approaches - Allele-specific silencing - Gene correction

    RNA-based therapies: - Antisense oligonucleotides - RNA interference - mRNA therapies

    Immunotherapies¶

    Active and passive immunization: - Antibody-based approaches - Targeting protein aggregates - Modulating neuroinflammation

    Early Detection¶

    Preclinical identification: - Biomarkers before symptoms - Risk stratification - Preventive interventions

    Notes¶

    • Neurodegenerative diseases are progressive and currently incurable
    • Protein aggregation is a common pathological feature
    • Metabolic disorders, especially obesity, are major modifiable risk factors
    • Neurovascular unit dysfunction is increasingly recognized as central to pathogenesis
    • Early intervention before symptom onset may be most effective
    • Lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, sleep) have neuroprotective effects
    • New therapeutic approaches target underlying disease mechanisms, not just symptoms
    • The "convergent-cascade" model explains how multiple factors interact to cause neurodegeneration
