    Bonds¶

    Overview¶

    Bonds are debt securities that represent loans made by investors to borrowers (typically governments or corporations). When you buy a bond, you're essentially lending money to the issuer in exchange for periodic interest payments and the return of the bond's face value when it matures.

    Basic Structure¶

    Principal (Face Value)¶

    • Loan Amount: The amount borrowed and repaid at maturity
    • Par Value: Typically $1,000 for corporate bonds and $100 for government bonds
    • Maturity Value: Amount returned to bondholder when bond reaches maturity

    Coupon Rate¶

    • Interest Rate: Fixed annual interest rate paid to bondholder
    • Payment Frequency: Typically semi-annual payments (every 6 months)
    • Income Stream: Provides predictable income throughout the bond's life

    Maturity Date¶

    • Term Length: Time until the bond reaches its full term
    • Repayment: Principal amount is returned to bondholder
    • Range: Can be short-term (1-3 years), medium-term (4-10 years), or long-term (10+ years)

    Types of Bonds¶

    Government Bonds¶

    • U.S. Treasury Bonds: Backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government
    • Municipal Bonds: Issued by state and local governments
    • Agency Bonds: Issued by government-sponsored enterprises
    • Safety: Generally considered the safest bond investments

    Corporate Bonds¶

    • Investment Grade: Higher credit quality, lower default risk
    • High Yield (Junk Bonds): Lower credit quality, higher default risk, higher yields
    • Sector Diversity: Available across various industries and companies
    • Risk-Return Trade-off: Higher yields for higher risk

    Municipal Bonds¶

    • Tax Advantages: Often exempt from federal income taxes
    • State/Local Taxes: May also be exempt from state and local taxes
    • Public Projects: Fund infrastructure, schools, and other public services
    • Yield Comparison: Often provide tax-equivalent yields higher than taxable bonds

    International Bonds¶

    • Foreign Government Bonds: Bonds issued by other countries
    • Global Corporate Bonds: Bonds from companies outside your home country
    • Currency Risk: Value affected by exchange rate fluctuations
    • Diversification: Provides geographic diversification for bond portfolios

    Bond Characteristics¶

    Credit Quality¶

    • Rating Agencies: Moody's, S&P, and Fitch assign credit ratings
    • Investment Grade: AAA to BBB- (higher quality, lower risk)
    • High Yield: BB+ to D (lower quality, higher risk)
    • Default Risk: Probability of issuer failing to make payments

    Yield¶

    • Coupon Yield: Annual interest payment divided by bond price
    • Current Yield: Annual interest divided by current market price
    • Yield to Maturity: Total return if held to maturity, including interest and price changes
    • Yield Curve: Relationship between bond yields and their maturities

    Duration and Convexity¶

    • Duration: Measures sensitivity to interest rate changes
    • Convexity: Measures how duration changes as interest rates change
    • Interest Rate Risk: Higher duration means greater sensitivity to rate changes
    • Portfolio Management: Used to manage interest rate risk in bond portfolios

    Bond Markets¶

    Primary Market¶

    • New Issues: Bonds sold when first issued by the issuer
    • Underwriting: Investment banks help issue and sell bonds
    • Pricing: Determined by market conditions and issuer creditworthiness
    • Distribution: Bonds sold to initial investors

    Secondary Market¶

    • Trading: Existing bonds bought and sold between investors
    • Liquidity: Some bonds are more liquid than others
    • Price Discovery: Market determines current bond prices
    • Transparency: Prices and yields are publicly available

    Investment Strategies¶

    Buy and Hold¶

    • Maturity Strategy: Hold bonds until maturity to receive full principal
    • Income Focus: Focus on regular interest payments
    • Stability: Provides predictable income and return of principal
    • Lower Trading Costs: Minimizes transaction costs and market timing risk

    Bond Laddering¶

    • Staggered Maturities: Bonds with different maturity dates
    • Liquidity Management: Regular access to maturing bonds
    • Reinvestment Risk: Reduces risk from reinvesting at unfavorable rates
    • Income Stability: Provides consistent income stream

    Bond Swapping¶

    • Tax-Loss Harvesting: Sell losing bonds to realize losses, buy similar bonds
    • Improving Yield: Swap bonds for higher-yielding alternatives
    • Upgrading Quality: Replace lower-quality bonds with higher-quality ones
    • Duration Management: Adjust portfolio sensitivity to interest rates

    Risk Factors¶

    Interest Rate Risk¶

    • Price Sensitivity: Bond prices move inversely to interest rates
    • Longer Duration: More sensitive to rate changes
    • Reinvestment Risk: New investments may earn lower rates
    • Strategy: Use duration matching to manage interest rate exposure

    Credit Risk¶

    • Default Risk: Probability of issuer failing to make payments
    • Recovery Rate: Amount recovered in case of default
    • Credit Spreads: Additional yield for taking on credit risk
    • Diversification: Spread credit risk across different issuers

    Inflation Risk¶

    • Purchasing Power: Inflation erodes the real value of bond payments
    • TIPS Protection: Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities adjust for inflation
    • Floating Rate Bonds: Interest rates adjust with inflation
    • Strategy: Include inflation-protected securities in portfolio

    Liquidity Risk¶

    • Marketability: Ability to buy or sell bonds quickly
    • Price Impact: Large trades may affect market prices
    • Bid-Ask Spread: Difference between buying and selling prices
    • Strategy: Focus on liquid bonds for trading flexibility

    Portfolio Considerations¶

    Asset Allocation¶

    • Risk Management: Bonds provide stability and income
    • Diversification: Different from stocks, providing portfolio balance
    • Income Generation: Regular cash flow for living expenses or reinvestment
    • Capital Preservation: Help protect portfolio during market downturns

    Duration Management¶

    • Interest Rate Outlook: Adjust duration based on rate expectations
    • Portfolio Balance: Mix of short and long duration bonds
    • Rebalancing: Adjust duration as market conditions change
    • Risk Control: Limit overall portfolio duration to manage risk

    Credit Quality¶

    • Risk Tolerance: Match credit quality to investor risk preferences
    • Income Needs: Higher credit quality for stability, lower for higher income
    • Economic Conditions: Adjust credit quality based on economic outlook
    • Diversification: Spread across different credit qualities and sectors
