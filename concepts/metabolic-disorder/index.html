    Metabolic disorder¶

    Metabolic disorders are a group of conditions characterized by abnormalities in metabolism—the biochemical processes that the body uses to convert food into energy. These disorders often involve dysregulation of glucose, lipids, or protein metabolism and are major risk factors for cardiovascular disease, neurodegenerative-disease, and other chronic conditions. Metabolic disorders cause widespread dysfunction of the neurovascular-unit and contribute to brain aging and cognitive decline.

    Types of Metabolic Disorders¶

    Obesity¶

    Definition: - Excessive accumulation of body fat - BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² (clinical definition) - More accurately measured by body composition and fat distribution

    Types: - Visceral obesity: Intra-abdominal fat surrounding organs - Strongest predictor of metabolic dysfunction - More inflammatory than subcutaneous fat - Primary driver of neurodegenerative cascade - Subcutaneous obesity: Fat beneath the skin - Less metabolically harmful - May have protective effects (metabolically healthy obesity)

    Pathophysiology: - Chronic low-grade inflammation from adipose tissue - Adipokine dysregulation (leptin, adiponectin) - Insulin resistance - Endothelial dysfunction

    Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)¶

    Definition: - Hyperglycemia due to insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency - Progressive loss of β-cell function

    Mechanisms: - Insulin resistance in muscle, liver, adipose tissue - Impaired insulin secretion from pancreatic β-cells - Increased hepatic glucose production - Chronic inflammation

    Complications: - Microvascular: retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy - Macrovascular: cardiovascular disease, stroke - Cognitive decline and dementia

    Metabolic Syndrome¶

    Definition: - Cluster of conditions increasing cardiovascular disease risk - Requires 3 of 5 diagnostic criteria

    Diagnostic criteria (ATP III): 1. Abdominal obesity (waist circumference >102 cm men, >88 cm women) 2. Triglycerides ≥ 150 mg/dL or on treatment 3. HDL cholesterol <40 mg/dL men, <50 mg/dL women or on treatment 4. Blood pressure ≥ 130/85 mmHg or on treatment 5. Fasting glucose ≥ 100 mg/dL or on treatment

    Pathophysiology: - Insulin resistance as central feature - Visceral adiposity as driver - Chronic inflammation - Prothrombotic state

    Dyslipidemia¶

    Atherogenic dyslipidemia: - Elevated triglycerides - Low HDL cholesterol - Small, dense LDL particles - Highly atherogenic

    Causes: - Insulin resistance - Obesity - Genetic factors - Poor diet

    Pathophysiology¶

    Insulin Resistance¶

    Mechanisms: 1. Inflammatory signaling: - TNF-α, IL-6 activate inflammatory kinases (JNK, IKKβ) - Serine phosphorylation of IRS-1 impairs insulin signaling

    1. Lipid accumulation:
    2. Ectopic fat deposition in liver, muscle
    3. Diacylglycerol (DAG) activates PKC

    4. Ceramide accumulation impairs signaling

    5. Mitochondrial dysfunction:

    6. Reduced oxidative capacity
    7. Increased ROS production

    8. Impaired fatty acid oxidation

    9. Endoplasmic reticulum stress:

    10. Unfolded protein response
    11. JNK activation
    12. Impaired protein folding

    Consequences: - Hyperglycemia - Hyperinsulinemia - Dyslipidemia - Endothelial dysfunction - Increased cardiovascular risk

    Chronic Inflammation¶

    Adipose tissue inflammation: - Macrophage infiltration - Cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) - Adipokine dysregulation

    Systemic effects: - Elevated CRP, fibrinogen - Endothelial activation - Insulin resistance - Atherosclerosis promotion

    Endothelial Dysfunction¶

    Mechanisms: - Reduced nitric oxide (NO) bioavailability - Increased oxidative stress - Pro-inflammatory state - Impaired vasodilation

    Consequences: - Atherosclerosis - Hypertension - Reduced tissue perfusion - Impaired neurovascular coupling

    Impact on the Brain¶

    Neurovascular Unit Dysfunction¶

    Blood-brain barrier breakdown: - Systemic inflammation disrupts tight junctions - Peripheral immune cell infiltration - Increased BBB permeability

    Impaired neurovascular coupling: - Reduced cerebral blood flow - Impaired metabolic support - Neuronal energy deficits

    Glymphatic dysfunction: - Impaired waste clearance - Accumulation of protein aggregates - Reduced nutrient delivery

    See: neurovascular-unit for detailed mechanisms

    Hypothalamic Dysfunction¶

    Hypothalamic inflammation: - Early response to metabolic overload - Microglial activation and gliosis - Neuronal injury

    Leptin and insulin resistance: - Impaired signaling in hypothalamus - Disrupted energy balance - Hyperphagia and weight gain

    Resetting of defended body weight: - Inflammation alters neuronal circuits - Raises adiposity set-point - Promotes weight maintenance at higher levels

    See: hypothalamus for detailed discussion

    Neurodegeneration¶

    Accelerated brain aging: - Structural brain changes - Reduced brain volume - White matter hyperintensities

    Increased dementia risk: - Alzheimer's disease - Vascular dementia - Parkinson's disease

    Mechanisms: - Chronic inflammation - Vascular dysfunction - Impaired protein clearance - Neuronal energy deficits

    See: neurodegenerative-disease for detailed discussion

    Risk Factors¶

    Genetic¶

    Polygenic risk: - Multiple genes with small effects - GWAS-identified variants - Polygenic risk scores

    Monogenic disorders: - Monogenic obesity (leptin, leptin receptor mutations) - MODY (maturity-onset diabetes of the young) - Familial hypercholesterolemia

    Lifestyle¶

    Diet: - High-calorie, ultra-processed foods - High saturated fat, sugar intake - Low fiber, micronutrient deficiency

    Physical inactivity: - Sedentary lifestyle - Reduced energy expenditure - Muscle loss (sarcopenia)

    Sleep: - Sleep deprivation - Circadian disruption - Sleep apnea

    Stress: - Chronic psychological stress - Cortisol dysregulation - Emotional eating

    Environmental¶

    Endocrine disruptors: - Bisphenol A (BPA) - Phthalates - Pesticides

    Microbiome: - Gut microbiota dysbiosis - Altered metabolite production - Increased intestinal permeability

    Clinical Assessment¶

    Anthropometric¶

    Body mass index (BMI): - Weight (kg) / height² (m²) - Underweight <18.5, Normal 18.5-24.9, Overweight 25-29.9, Obese ≥30 - Limited: doesn't distinguish fat vs muscle, distribution

    Waist circumference: - Visceral adiposity indicator - >102 cm men, >88 cm women (increased risk)

    Waist-to-hip ratio: - Waist circumference / hip circumference - >0.9 men, >0.85 women (increased risk)

    Biochemical¶

    Glucose metabolism: - Fasting glucose - HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin) - Oral glucose tolerance test - Insulin levels, HOMA-IR

    Lipids: - Total cholesterol - LDL cholesterol - HDL cholesterol - Triglycerides - Apolipoprotein B

    Inflammation: - C-reactive protein (CRP) - Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) - Cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α)

    Liver function: - ALT, AST (elevated in NAFLD) - GGT

    Imaging¶

    Body composition: - DEXA scan (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) - MRI/CT for visceral fat quantification

    Organ damage: - Echocardiogram (cardiac function) - Carotid ultrasound (atherosclerosis) - MRI brain (white matter hyperintensities)

    Therapeutic Approaches¶

    Lifestyle Modifications¶

    Dietary interventions: - Caloric restriction for weight loss - Mediterranean diet - DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension) - Reduced sugar and refined carbohydrates - Increased fiber, whole grains, fruits, vegetables

    Physical activity: - Aerobic exercise (150 min/week moderate) - Resistance training (2-3 sessions/week) - Reduce sedentary time

    Weight management: - 5-10% weight loss produces significant health benefits - Sustainable weight maintenance - Bariatric surgery for severe obesity

    Sleep optimization: - 7-9 hours per night - Treat sleep apnea - Regular sleep schedule

    Pharmacological¶

    Diabetes: - Metformin (first-line) - SGLT2 inhibitors - GLP-1 receptor agonists - DPP-4 inhibitors - Insulin

    Dyslipidemia: - Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) - Ezetimibe - PCSK9 inhibitors - Fibrates

    Hypertension: - ACE inhibitors/ARBs - Calcium channel blockers - Thiazide diuretics - Beta-blockers

    Weight management: - GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide) - Orlistat - Phentermine/topiramate - Naltrexone/bupropion

    Bariatric Surgery¶

    Procedures: - Roux-en-Y gastric bypass - Sleeve gastrectomy - Adjustable gastric banding

    Benefits: - Significant, sustained weight loss - Remission of T2DM in many patients - Improved cardiovascular risk factors - Reduced mortality

    Mechanisms: - Restriction and malabsorption - Gut hormone changes (GLP-1, PYY) - Altered food preferences

    Prevention¶

    Primary prevention: - Healthy diet - Regular physical activity - Weight maintenance - Avoid smoking, excess alcohol - Stress management

    Secondary prevention: - Early detection through screening - Aggressive risk factor modification - Targeted interventions

    Tertiary prevention: - Complication management - Organ protection - Quality of life improvement

    Notes¶

    • Metabolic disorders are interrelated and often co-occur
    • Insulin resistance is the central pathophysiological feature
    • Chronic inflammation is a key driver of complications
    • Visceral adiposity is more harmful than subcutaneous fat
    • Metabolic disorders are major modifiable risk factors for neurodegeneration
    • Lifestyle interventions are first-line and highly effective
    • Early intervention prevents progression and complications
    • The brain is particularly vulnerable to metabolic dysfunction
    • Hypothalamic inflammation is an early event in obesity
    • Neurovascular unit dysfunction mediates many brain effects
