# Genome-wide association meta-analysis of childhood ADHD symptoms and diagnosis identifies new loci and potential effector genes

## Study Overview
**Title**: Genome-wide association meta-analysis of childhood ADHD symptoms and diagnosis identifies new loci and potential effector genes  
**Journal**: Nature Genetics, Volume 57, October 2025 | 2427–2435  
**DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02295-y  
**Authors**: Camiel M. van der Laan et al. (124 authors across 28 cohorts)  
**Publication Date**: September 17, 2025 (online), October 2025 (print)

## Key Study Details
- **Sample Size**: 70,953 unique individuals with 290,134 ADHD symptom measures from 28 cohorts
- **ADHD Diagnosis Cohort**: 38,691 cases and 186,843 controls
- **Study Design**: Genome-wide association meta-analysis (GWAMA) combining quantitative ADHD symptom measures with clinical ADHD diagnosis
- **Focus**: Childhood ADHD symptoms and diagnosis across multiple raters, ages, and instruments

## Main Findings

### 1. ADHD Symptoms vs Diagnosis Analysis
- **ADHD Symptoms Alone**: No genome-wide significant variants found
  - SNP heritability (h²SNP): 0.04 (s.e. = 0.01)
  - Genetic correlation with ADHD diagnosis: rg = 1.00 (s.e. = 0.06)
  
- **Combined Analysis (ADHD Symptoms + Diagnosis)**: 
  - 39 independent loci identified
  - 17 loci were newly discovered
  - SNP heritability: 0.11 (s.e. = 0.01)
  - 2,039 genome-wide significant variants (P < 5 × 10⁻⁸)

### 2. Genetic Architecture
- **Strong Genetic Correlation**: rg = 1.00 between ADHD symptoms and diagnosis
- **Support for Continuum Model**: Clinical ADHD represents the extreme end of a continuous genetic liability indexed by ADHD symptoms
- **Polygenic Nature**: ADHD is highly polygenic with many contributing genetic variants

### 3. Gene Discovery and Pathway Analysis
- **FLAMES Analysis**: Identified 22 potential ADHD effector genes
  - 14 genes also tagged by MAGMA gene-based test
  - 10 genes previously reported in ADHD literature
  - 8 genes newly linked to ADHD (not in GWAS Catalog)

- **New ADHD Risk Genes**: EMCN, STK32C, PCDH17, TCF12, PEAK1, IGF1R, CTNNA2, ABCA12

- **Pathway Enrichment**: 
  - Frontal cortex expression
  - Synaptic pathways (postsynaptic density, synapse organization)
  - Transcription factor targets and microRNA targets

## Polygenic Score Performance
Three cohorts tested (ALSPAC, MoBa, NTR):
- **ADHDOVERALL (Combined)**: Best performance (0.3-3.1% variance explained)
- **ADHDDIAG (Diagnosis only)**: Moderate performance (0.2-2.5% variance explained)  
- **ADHDSYMP (Symptoms only)**: Lowest performance (0.1-1.2% variance explained)

**Key Finding**: Combining ADHD symptoms with diagnosis improves polygenic risk score prediction accuracy.

## Methodological Details
### Study Design
- **Cohorts**: 28 population-based cohorts from ACTION and EAGLE consortia
- **Age Range**: 1.5-18 years (with retrospective adult assessments)
- **Raters**: Mothers, fathers, teachers, self-reports
- **Instruments**: 20 different ADHD assessment instruments
- **Quality Control**: Rigorous QC including LDSC intercept analysis

### Statistical Methods
- **Meta-analysis**: N-weighted multivariate GWAMA
- **Gene Mapping**: FUMA, FINEMAP, CAVIARBF, PAINTOR
- **Effector Gene Identification**: FLAMES (Fine-mapped Locus Assessment Model of Effector genes)
- **Pathway Analysis**: MAGMA gene-set analysis, FUMA GENE2FUNC
- **Genetic Correlations**: LDSC in genomicSEM

### Sample Characteristics
- **Ethnicity**: Primarily European ancestry (due to data availability)
- **Sex**: Included as covariate in all analyses
- **Age**: Stratified analysis across developmental stages

## Implications and Significance

### Clinical Implications
1. **Continuum Model**: Supports the view that clinical ADHD represents the extreme end of a continuous distribution of ADHD symptoms
2. **Early Identification**: ADHD symptom measures can help identify genetic risk before full clinical diagnosis
3. **Polygenic Risk**: Combined symptom and diagnosis data improves genetic risk prediction

### Research Implications
1. **Novel Genes**: 8 new ADHD risk genes identified, expanding understanding of biological pathways
2. **Synaptic Function**: Enrichment in synaptic pathways supports neurodevelopmental hypotheses
3. **Pleiotropy**: Strong genetic correlations with other psychiatric and behavioral traits

### Limitations
1. **Population Bias**: Primarily European ancestry limits generalizability
2. **Age Focus**: Childhood-focused; adult ADHD less represented
3. **Heterogeneity**: Different assessment instruments may introduce measurement variance

## Future Directions
1. **Diverse Populations**: Include non-European ancestry samples
2. **Longitudinal Studies**: Track genetic effects across development
3. **Functional Validation**: Experimental studies of novel effector genes
4. **Clinical Translation**: Polygenic risk scores for early intervention

## Conclusion
This large-scale meta-analysis significantly advances our understanding of ADHD genetics by:
- Identifying 17 new risk loci
- Discovering 8 novel ADHD effector genes
- Demonstrating the value of combining symptom and diagnosis data
- Providing comprehensive genetic correlations with other traits
- Supporting the continuum model of ADHD as a quantitative trait

The findings highlight the polygenic nature of ADHD and provide new targets for understanding the biological mechanisms underlying this common neurodevelopmental disorder.
