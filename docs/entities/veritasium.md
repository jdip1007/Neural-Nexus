# Veritasium

## Overview

**Veritasium** is a popular science YouTube channel created and hosted by [[derek-muller]]. The channel produces high-quality educational videos exploring scientific concepts, often through experiments, interviews with experts, and visual explanations. As of July 2026, the channel has over 21 million subscribers.^[raw/videos/one-billion-heartbeats-veritasium.md]

## Key Facts

- **Host:** Derek Muller (primary), Henry van Dyck (co-host for some videos)
- **Subscribers:** 21 million+ (as of July 2026)
- **Content:** Science education, experiments, expert interviews
- **Style:** In-depth explanations, visual demonstrations, scientific accuracy
- **Sponsorship:** Often sponsored by Brilliant (educational platform)

## Video Style and Approach

### Educational Philosophy
- **Deep dives:** Comprehensive coverage of topics (often 10-30+ minutes)
- **Expert consultation:** Videos feature leading researchers and academics
- **Visual learning:** Animations, demonstrations, and experiments
- **Scientific accuracy:** Careful fact-checking and consultation

### Typical Structure
1. **Engaging hook:** Surprising fact or question
2. **Historical context:** Background and development of idea
3. **Expert interviews:** Leading researchers explain concepts
4. **Mathematical derivations:** Clear explanations of equations
5. **Visual demonstrations:** Animations and experiments
6. **Scientific debate:** Presenting multiple viewpoints
7. **Applications and extensions:** Real-world implications

### Expert Collaboration
Videos often feature extensive consultation with experts:
- **Primary experts** provide detailed explanations
- **Critics** present alternative viewpoints
- **Multiple contributors** ensure scientific accuracy
- **Research teams** gather data and create visualizations

## Notable Content

### Biological Scaling Video
**"Why does every mammal get 1 billion heartbeats in their life?"** (July 25, 2026)^[raw/videos/one-billion-heartbeats-veritasium.md]

- Mathematical derivations and explanations
- Historical context (Kleiber's Law, Surface Law)
- WBE theory explanation
- Scientific debate presentation
