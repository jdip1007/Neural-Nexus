# Geoffrey West

## Overview

**Geoffrey West** is a theoretical physicist and complex systems researcher best known for co-developing the **WBE Theory** (West-Brown-Enquist theory) of biological scaling and authoring the book "Scale," which examines scaling laws across biology, cities, and companies. His work bridges physics, biology, and social sciences, demonstrating universal scaling principles that govern diverse complex systems.^[raw/videos/one-billion-heartbeats-veritasium.md]

## Key Facts

- **Field:** Theoretical physics, complex systems
- **Affiliation:** Santa Fe Institute (and others)
- **Major Contribution:** Co-developer of WBE theory explaining biological scaling
- **Book:** "Scale" - comprehensive treatment of scaling laws across domains
- **Significance:** Demonstrated that mathematical laws govern biological and social systems

## Major Contributions

### WBE Theory (West-Brown-Enquist)
- **Collaboration:** With James Brown and Brian Enquist
- **Goal:** Provide mechanistic explanation for Kleiber's Law
- **Key insight:** Fractal distribution networks in organisms produce 1/4 power scaling
- **Impact:** Established theoretical foundation for biological scaling^[raw/videos/one-billion-heartbeats-veritasium.md]

### Book: "Scale"

## Key Ideas and Concepts

### Universal Scaling Laws
Different systems show similar scaling patterns:
- **Biology:** Metabolic rate ∝ M^(3/4)
- **Cities:** Infrastructure scales predictably with population
- **Companies:** Growth and performance follow power laws
- **Social networks:** Connection patterns scale systematically

### Fractal Networks
Fundamental mechanism underlying scaling:
- Space-filling patterns in organisms
- Efficient resource distribution
- Minimization principles
- Self-similarity across scales

### Emergent Behavior
Complex patterns emerge from simple rules:
- Individual organism design leads to universal scaling
- City structure leads to predictable scaling
- System-level properties not obvious from individual components

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| West alone developed WBE theory | West worked with Brown and Enquist; WBE stands for West-Brown-Enquist |
| WBE theory is the only explanation for scaling | WBE is one of several mechanisms; debate exists about universality^[raw/videos/one-billion-heartbeats-veritasium.md] |
| Scaling laws are exact predictions | Scaling laws describe central tendencies with variation and exceptions |
| West claims scaling is universal | West demonstrates universal tendencies, acknowledges variation |
