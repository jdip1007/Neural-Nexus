# Psychiatric Comorbidity

**Psychiatric comorbidity** is the coexistence of multiple psychiatric conditions in the same individual. It is highly prevalent (27.9% of adolescents meet criteria for multiple disorders), leads to poorer outcomes, and suggests shared neurobiological origins across different psychopathologies.^[raw/articles/shared-neural-basis-psychiatric-comorbidity.md]

## Definition

The simultaneous presence of two or more psychiatric diagnoses in one person. Not merely co-occurrence — comorbid disorders often share risk factors, neural circuits, and genetic variants, suggesting common etiological mechanisms rather than independent pathologies.

## Prevalence and Impact

- **27.9%** of adolescents aged 14–17 meet multiple diagnostic criteria (population-based data)
- Peak onset in adolescence for both externalizing and internalizing disorders
- Comorbid individuals experience: poorer outcomes, severe cognitive deficits, longer illness duration, higher treatment resistance
- Traditional case-control studies treat comorbidity as a confound to remove — the NP factor approach treats it as the phenomenon to explain

## Externalizing vs. Internalizing Dimensions

| Dimension | Disorders | Onset Pattern | Overlap |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|---------|
| **Externalizing** | ASD, ADHD, ODD, CD | Behavioral dysregulation | 59–78% shared neural edges |
| **Internalizing** | GAD, Depression, ED, SP | Emotional distress | 44–67% shared neural edges |
| **Cross-group** | — | — | 20–37% shared neural edges |

Cross-group overlap (20–37%) is lower but substantial — this is the territory the [neuropsychopathological-factor](concepts/neuropsychopathological-factor.md) explains.

## Neurobiological Explanation

The [neuropsychopathological-factor](concepts/neuropsychopathological-factor.md) provides a mechanistic account:
1. **Genetic risk**: ADHD and depression PRS → neurodevelopmental SNPs
2. **Delayed development**: Prefrontal circuits mature slower
3. **Weak connectivity**: SMF, SAL, FPN networks show reduced FC
4. **Executive deficits**: Poor inhibition, working memory, risk adjustment
5. **Broad vulnerability**: Same neural deficit manifests as different symptoms depending on context

This chain explains *why* comorbidity is so common: one neural vulnerability → multiple symptom expressions.

## Clinical Implications

1. **Early screening**: NP factor scores at age 14 predict symptom persistence to age 19
2. **Transdiagnostic treatment**: Target executive function rather than individual diagnoses
3. **Critical window**: Adolescent prefrontal maturation is the window for intervention
4. **Prevention**: Higher NP factor scores → slower normalization → chronic risk

## Traditional vs. Transdiagnostic Approaches

| Approach | View of Comorbidity | Treatment Logic |
|----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Traditional** | Confound to remove | Treat each disorder separately |
| **p factor** | Statistical commonality | Address general distress |
| **NP factor (transdiagnostic)** | Shared neural mechanism | Target prefrontal circuits and executive function |

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| Comorbidity = random co-occurrence | Shared neurobiological origins explain co-occurrence rates |
| Each disorder needs separate treatment | Shared neural mechanism suggests transdiagnostic interventions |
| Comorbidity is rare | 27.9% of adolescents meet multiple criteria |
| Externalizing and internalizing are unrelated | 20–37% shared neural edges across groups |
