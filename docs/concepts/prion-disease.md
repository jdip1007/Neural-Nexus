# Prion Disease

## Definition
Prion diseases (transmissible spongiform encephalopathies, TSEs) are a group of fatal neurodegenerative disorders caused by misfolded proteins called prions. Unlike conventional pathogens, prions contain no nucleic acid and consist primarily of an abnormally folded isoform of a normal cellular protein.^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

## Core Mechanism

### Protein-Only Infectivity
Prions are "proteinaceous infectious particles" that propagate their misfolded conformation without DNA or RNA. The key mechanism involves:
- A normal cellular protein (PrP^C) misfolding into an abnormal isoform (PrP^Sc)
- PrP^Sc acts as a template, converting normal proteins into the misfolded form
- Accumulation leads to tissue damage, particularly in the brain^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

### Spongiform Pathology
- Brain tissue develops characteristic "spongy" holes
- Neuronal loss and gliosis
- Amyloid plaque formation from aggregated prions
- Widespread neuronal degeneration

## Historical Prion Diseases

### Kuru (New Guinea)
- **Discovery**: Documented in the Eastern Highlands of Papua New Guinea^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Transmission**: Linked to endocannibalistic funeral practices
- **Pathology**: Similar to other TSEs, with spongiform changes in cerebellum and basal ganglia
- **Significance**: First documented human prion disease transmitted between people
- **Extinction**: Declined with cessation of cannibalistic practices^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

### Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD)
- **First Descriptions**: 1920 - Creutzfeldt described focal CNS disease; Jakob reported peculiar diseases with remarkable anatomical findings^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Forms**:
  - Sporadic CJD (most common, ~85% of cases)
  - Familial CJD (genetic mutations in PRNP gene)
  - Iatrogenic CJD (medical transmission)
  - Variant CJD (linked to BSE exposure)
- **Symptoms**: Rapidly progressive dementia, myoclonus, visual disturbances, ataxia
- **Prognosis**: Always fatal, typically within months^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

### Scrapie (Sheep/Goats)
- **First Described**: 18th century in sheep
- **Heat Stability**: Studies show scrapie agent survives conditions that destroy conventional pathogens^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Chromatographic Behavior**: Distinct from known viruses and bacteria^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Significance**: First recognized animal prion disease

### Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy (BSE / Mad Cow Disease)
- **First Described**: 1987 - "Novel progressive spongiform encephalopathy in cattle"^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Transmission**: Contaminated meat-and-bone meal in cattle feed
- **Human Impact**: Variant CJD linked to BSE exposure
- **Control**: Bans on ruminant protein in feed

### Yeast Prions
- **URE3**: Altered URE2 protein in Saccharomyces cerevisiae^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Significance**: Evidence for prion-like mechanisms in simple organisms
- **Research Value**: Model systems for studying protein misfolding^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

## Clinical Manifestations

### Common Symptoms
- Rapid cognitive decline and memory loss
- Behavioral changes and personality alterations
- Motor dysfunction (ataxia, myoclonus)
- Visual disturbances
- Difficulty with coordination and balance^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

### Disease Progression
- **Early Stage**: Memory loss, confusion, behavioral changes
- **Middle Stage**: Ataxia, myoclonus, visual problems, significant cognitive impairment
- **Late Stage**: Severe dementia, loss of motor function, akinetic mutism
- **Outcome**: Death typically within months to 2 years^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]

## Epidemiology

### Human Prion Diseases
- **Incidence**: 1-2 cases per million population per year worldwide
- **Age Distribution**: Typically older adults (median ~65 years)
- **Forms**: Sporadic (85%), familial (10-15%), acquired (1-2%)
- **Geography**: Worldwide distribution with some regional variations

### Animal Prion Diseases
- **Scrapie**: Widespread in sheep and goats
- **BSE**: Major outbreak in UK (1980s-1990s), controlled worldwide
- **Chronic Wasting Disease**: Deer, elk, moose - spreading in North America
- **Other Species**: Mink, cats, exotic ungulates

## Pathophysiology

### Molecular Mechanism
- **PrP^C**: Normal cellular prion protein (function unclear, possibly neuroprotective)
- **PrP^Sc**: Pathogenic isoform with different secondary structure (more β-sheet)
- **Conversion**: PrP^Sc templates conformational change of PrP^C
- **Accumulation**: Aggregates form amyloid plaques and neuronal damage

### Cellular Pathology
- **Neuronal Loss**: Widespread death of neurons
- **Spongiform Change**: Vacuolation of neuropil
- **Gliosis**: Astrocyte and microglial activation
- **Amyloid Plaques**: Prion protein deposits in brain tissue

### Transmission Routes
- **Direct**: Contact with infected tissue (e.g., medical procedures)
- **Dietary**: Consumption of infected material (e.g., BSE, kuru)
- **Iatrogenic**: Contaminated surgical instruments, dura mater grafts, growth hormone
- **Genetic**: Inherited mutations in PRNP gene

## Diagnosis

### Clinical Criteria
- Rapid progressive dementia
- Myoclonus or other characteristic movements
- Ataxia or visual disturbances
- Exclusion of other causes

### Diagnostic Tests
- **MRI**: Characteristic cortical ribboning, basal ganglia changes^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **EEG**: Periodic sharp wave complexes (in sporadic CJD)
- **CSF**: 14-3-3 protein, tau protein elevation
- **Biopsy**: Brain tissue (spongiform changes, PrP immunostaining)
- **Post-Mortem**: Definitive diagnosis

### Differential Diagnosis
- Alzheimer's disease
- Parkinson's disease with dementia
- Lewy body dementia
- Vascular dementia
- Other rapidly progressive dementias

## Treatment

### Current Status
- **No Cure**: No effective treatment to stop or reverse disease progression^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **Supportive Care**: Symptom management, comfort measures
- **Experimental Approaches**: Prion inhibitors, immunotherapies (research stage)

### Prevention
- **BSE Controls**: Bans on ruminant protein in feed
- **Medical Sterilization**: Strict protocols for surgical instruments
- **Blood Donor Screening**: Deferral for at-risk individuals
- **Genetic Counseling**: For familial forms

## Research & Scientific History

### Key Discoveries
- **1957**: Kuru documented in New Guinea^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **1972**: Hadlow notes similarities between kuru and scrapie^[raw/videos/prion-disease-colleague-story.md]
- **1982**: Prusiner proposes "prion" hypothesis
- **1987**: Nobel Prize awarded to Prusiner for prion research
- **1996**: Variant CJD linked to BSE
