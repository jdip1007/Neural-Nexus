# Vyvanse (Lisdexamfetamine Dimesylate)

## Overview

Vyvanse (lisdexamfetamine dimesylate) is a central nervous system (CNS) stimulant indicated for the treatment of:
- Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) in adults and pediatric patients (ages 6-17)
- Moderate to Severe Binge Eating Disorder (BED) in adults

**Classification**: Schedule II controlled substance (CII) due to high potential for abuse and dependence.

## Clinical Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action
Lisdexamfetamine dimesylate is a prodrug that is converted to dextroamphetamine, a CNS stimulant. The exact mechanism of action in ADHD and BED is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve:
- Increased dopamine and norepinephrine neurotransmission
- Modulation of cortical and subcortical neural pathways

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption**: Following oral administration, lisdexamfetamine is absorbed in the small intestine and converted to dextroamphetamine
- **Metabolism**: Lisdexamfetamine is not metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes
- **Excretion**: Primarily renal excretion
- **No accumulation**: No accumulation of lisdexamfetamine and dextroamphetamine at steady state

## Indications and Usage

### ADHD
- **Adults and pediatric patients (6-17 years)**: Treatment of ADHD
- **Limitation of use**: Not indicated for weight loss

### Binge Eating Disorder
- **Adults**: Treatment of moderate to severe BED
- **Limitation of use**: Not indicated for weight loss or obesity treatment

## Dosage and Administration

### ADHD Treatment
| Patient Population | Initial Dose | Titration Schedule | Recommended Dose | Maximum Dose |
|-------------------|--------------|-------------------|------------------|--------------|
| Adults and Pediatric (6-17 years) | 30 mg daily | 10 mg or 20 mg weekly increases | 30-70 mg daily | 70 mg daily |

### BED Treatment
| Patient Population | Initial Dose | Titration Schedule | Recommended Dose | Maximum Dose |
|-------------------|--------------|-------------------|------------------|--------------|
| Adults | 30 mg daily | 20 mg weekly increases | 50-70 mg daily | 70 mg daily |

### Special Populations
- **Renal impairment**: 
  - Severe renal impairment (GFR 15-30 mL/min): Max 50 mg/day
  - ESRD (GFR < 15 mL/min): Max 30 mg/day

## Contraindications

1. **Known hypersensitivity** to amphetamine products or other ingredients of Vyvanse
2. **Hypertensive crisis** when used concomitantly with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)
3. **Advanced arteriosclerosis**
4. **Symptomatic cardiovascular disease**
5. **Moderate to severe hypertension**
6. **Hyperthyroidism**
7. **Glaucoma**
8. **Agitation or anxiety states**
9. **History of drug abuse**

## Warnings and Precautions

### 1. Abuse and Dependence
- High potential for abuse and dependence
- Assess risk of abuse before prescribing
- Monitor for signs of abuse and dependence during therapy
- Federal controlled substance (CII)

### 2. Serious Cardiovascular Reactions
- Sudden death, stroke, and myocardial infarction reported in adults
- Contraindicated in patients with structural cardiac abnormalities
- Monitor blood pressure and heart rate

### 3. Psychiatric Adverse Reactions
- May cause psychotic or manic symptoms in patients with no prior history
- May exacerbate symptoms in patients with pre-existing psychosis
- Evaluate for bipolar disorder prior to stimulant use

### 4. Increased Blood Pressure and Heart Rate
- May cause elevations in blood pressure and pulse rate
- Monitor cardiovascular status

### 5. Peripheral Vasculopathy
- Associated with peripheral vasculopathy, including Raynaud's phenomenon
- Monitor for digital changes

### 6. Suppression of Growth
- Monitor height and weight in pediatric patients
- Long-term treatment may cause temporary slowing in growth rate

### 7. Seizures
- May lower seizure threshold
- Use with caution in patients with history of seizures

## Drug Interactions

### Contraindicated
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)**: Hypertensive crisis risk

### Require Caution
- **Serotonergic drugs**: Increased risk of serotonin syndrome
- **Other CNS stimulants**: Additive cardiovascular effects
- **Alpha-2 agonists**: Monitor for blood pressure changes

### No Clinically Important Interactions
- Guanfacine
- Venlafaxine
- Omeprazole

## Use in Specific Populations

### Pregnancy
- No adequate well-controlled studies in pregnant women
- Animal studies show no adverse effects on embryo-fetal development
- Use only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus

### Lactation
- Amphetamines are excreted in human milk
- Discontinue drug or nursing, taking into account importance of drug to mother

### Pediatric Use
- **ADHD**: Safety and effectiveness established in ages 6-17 years
- **BED**: Safety and effectiveness not established in pediatric patients
- **Below age 6**: Safety and efficacy not established

### Geriatric Use
- Limited data in patients over 65 years
- Consider increased sensitivity and comorbidities

### Renal Impairment
- **Severe renal impairment**: Max 50 mg/day
- **ESRD**: Max 30 mg/day

## Overdose

### Signs and Symptoms
- Agitation, tremor, muscle twitching, convulsions
- Confusion, hallucinations, panic states
- Hyperreflexia, rapid respiration, tachycardia
- Hypertension, circulatory collapse

### Treatment
- **Gastric lavage** and **activated charcoal** if recent ingestion
- **Symptomatic treatment** and **supportive care**
- **Monitoring** of vital signs and mental status
- **Acidification of urine** may increase amphetamine excretion

## Patient Counseling

### Important Instructions
- Take medication exactly as prescribed
- Do not share with others
- Store in safe place, away from children
- Report any cardiovascular symptoms

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular blood pressure and heart rate monitoring
- Height and weight monitoring in children
- Psychiatric symptom assessment
- Signs of abuse or misuse

### Pregnancy and Lactation
- Inform healthcare provider if pregnant or planning pregnancy
- Consult healthcare provider before breastfeeding

## Clinical Studies

### ADHD Efficacy
- **Children (6-12 years)**: Significant improvement in ADHD symptoms vs placebo
- **Adolescents (13-17 years)**: Significant reduction in treatment failure rates
- **Adults**: Significant improvement in attention and reduction in symptoms

### BED Efficacy
- **Adults**: Significant reduction in binge eating episodes
- **Long-term**: Reduced relapse rates with continued treatment
