# ASD IQ Bimodal Distribution

**Definition:** The observation that IQ distributions in clinical ASD samples show two peaks — one at low IQ and one at high IQ — with fewer individuals in the average range than expected.

## Mechanism

Wolff et al. (2022) observed in specialized ASD outpatient clinics:
- **38.2%** below average intelligence (IQ < 85)
- **40%** above average intelligence (IQ > 115)
- **21.8%** average intelligence (IQ 85–115)

This bimodal pattern may reflect:
1. **True population structure:** ASD genuinely clusters at cognitive extremes, with different etiological pathways
2. **Referral filtering:** Average-IQ individuals with ASD may not seek specialized services (they function adequately without clinical support)
3. **Diagnostic threshold effects:** The "sweet spot" of visible-enough symptoms + impaired-enough functioning drives clinic presentation

## Dysfunction in Diagnostic Context

The bimodal distribution complicates:
- Norm-referenced assessment (no single "typical" ASD IQ profile)
- Support allocation (needs differ dramatically across the IQ spectrum)
- Research sampling (either tail can dominate depending on recruitment)

## Clinical Significance

- Service systems designed around "low-IQ ASD" miss the high-IQ cluster's needs
- High-IQ ASD individuals may have masked social difficulties but significant internal distress
- The "missing middle" (average IQ) may represent individuals who slip through both diagnostic and support systems
