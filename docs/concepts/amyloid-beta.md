# Amyloid-beta (Aβ)

## Definition

Amyloid-beta (Aβ) is a peptide derived from amyloid precursor protein (APP) that aggregates into insoluble plaques in the brain, serving as the primary constituent of neuritic plaques in  and other neurodegenerative diseases.^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Core Mechanism

### Production and Regulation
- Cleaved from APP sequentially by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Released from neurons in a manner modulated by synaptic activity^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Regulated by SORL1 (sortilin-related receptor-1), a neuronal receptor that controls APP trafficking to decrease Aβ conversion^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Loss-of-function SORL1 mutations are important genetic risk factors for AD^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Aggregation Pathway
Aβ monomers (particularly Aβ42, which is more aggregation-prone) self-aggregate through intermediate stages:
1. **Oligomers:** Small soluble aggregates
2. **Protofibrils:** Intermediate species (pathogenic; targeted by lecanemab)
3. **Mature fibrils:** Insoluble cross-β-pleated sheet structures
4. **Amyloid plaques:** Deposits in brain tissue

As plaques age, Aβ undergoes post-translational modifications:
- N-terminal pyroglutamate modification (pE3-Aβ; pathogenic; targeted by donanemab)
- Phosphorylation (pSer8Aβ)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Timeline
- Aβ accumulation begins in brain ~20 years before cognitive impairment onset^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Considered a critical first step in AD pathological cascade
- However, cognitive decline correlates more strongly with [tau-pathology](concepts/tau-pathology.md) than amyloid burden^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Structural Heterogeneity

### Cryo-EM Insights
 studies revealed disease-specific Aβ conformations with important implications:
- Sporadic AD Aβ42 filaments differ from familial AD filaments^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Both differ from Aβ40 filament structures^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Down syndrome-associated AD (DSAD) shows both shared and novel filaments^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Critical finding:** Brain-derived filaments differ markedly from synthetically derived fibrils in side-chain orientations, contacts, and packing^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Disease Modeling Implications
- Synthetic fibrils may not accurately model human disease^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- App<sup>NL-F</sup> knockin mouse Aβ42 filaments match human brain-derived filaments from DIAD (but caution in extrapolating to sporadic AD)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Pathogenic Species

### Clinical Trial Evidence
Therapeutic successes identify which Aβ species are most pathogenic:

| Aβ Species | Therapeutic Target | Clinical Outcome | Evidence |
|------------|-------------------|------------------|----------|
| **Protofibrils** | Lecanemab | ~30% slowing cognitive decline over 18 months | CLARITY-AD trial^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| **pE3-Aβ plaques** | Donanemab | Similar slowing of decline | TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 trial^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| **Monomers** | Solanezumab | Failed to show benefit | No clinical improvement^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| **Fibrils** | Aducanumab | Equivocal outcomes | Inconclusive evidence^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |

### Mechanism of Action
Anti-Aβ antibodies clear plaques via **Fc-mediated microglial activation and phagocytosis**^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- This explains why crenezumab (IgG4 with reduced effector function) failed^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Lecanemab and donanemab clear plaques more rapidly/robustly than earlier antibodies

## Therapeutic Effects

### Disease Modification
Amyloid removal produces:
- **Cognitive benefit:** ~30% slowing over 18 months^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Tau slowing:** Reduced tau pathology progression (measured by tau-PET and p-tau biomarkers)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Reduced neuroinflammation:** Attenuated GFAP and other inflammatory markers^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Dose-dependent effects:** Correlation between magnitude of amyloid removal and downstream benefits^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Limitations
- **Modest slowing:** 30% benefit vs robust amyloid clearance^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Co-pathologies persist:** Tau, neuroinflammation, lipid dyshomeostasis become semi-autonomous after amyloid seeding^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Earlier intervention better:** TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 stratified by tau-PET showed greater benefit with less advanced disease^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Current State

### Validated Hypothesis
After decades of failed anti-amyloid trials, 2023 marked a paradigm shift:
- CLARITY-AD and TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 validated that amyloid removal can slow AD^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Supports amyloid as causative driver of downstream pathologies^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Approved Therapies
- **Lecanemab:** Anti-Aβ protofibril antibody; approved for early AD
- **Donanemab:** Anti-pE3-Aβ antibody; approved for early AD

### Future Directions
- Complementary therapies targeting tau and neuroinflammation needed^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- CAR-based therapies (CAR macrophages, CAR-T cells, CAR astrocytes) targeting amyloid in preclinical development^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Preclinical trials testing prevention in asymptomatic at-risk individuals (AHEAD 3-45, TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 3)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Why It Matters

Aβ is the earliest detectable pathology in AD and a validated therapeutic target. Understanding its structural heterogeneity and pathogenic species enables more targeted diagnostics and therapeutics. The clinical success of anti-Aβ antibodies represents the first disease-modifying treatments for AD, transforming the therapeutic landscape from symptomatic management to genuine disease modification.

## Open Questions

1. **Species specificity:** Is therapeutic effect due to targeting specific Aβ species (protofibrils vs pE3-Aβ) or the speed/magnitude of clearance?^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

2. **Structural consequences:** What are the biological pathways activated by different Aβ conformations?

3. **Optimal timing:** How early must intervention begin to prevent cognitive decline?

4. **Combination therapies:** What complementary approaches (anti-tau, anti-inflammatory, lipid modulation) will provide additive/synergistic benefits?

5. **Prevention:** Can anti-amyloid therapies prevent AD in asymptomatic at-risk individuals?

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| Aβ plaques are the sole cause of AD | Aβ is initial trigger but co-pathologies (tau, inflammation, lipid dysregulation) drive cognitive decline |
| Any anti-Aβ antibody will work | Pathogenic species matter: protofibrils and pE3-Aβ are therapeutic targets, monomers are not |
| Amyloid removal should halt disease | Amyloid removal slows but doesn't stop progression due to semi-autonomous co-pathologies |
| Synthetic fibrils model human disease | Brain-derived filaments have different structures; synthetic fibrils may not accurately model disease |
| Cognitive decline correlates with amyloid burden | Cognitive decline correlates more strongly with tau pathology; amyloid is upstream trigger |
