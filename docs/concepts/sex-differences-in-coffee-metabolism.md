# Sex Differences in Coffee Metabolism

**Definition:** The pronounced sex-specificity in coffee's metabolic and hormonal associations, where men show broader metabolomic correlations, stronger glucose-insulin associations, and a full androgen-related hormonal profile, while women's associations are limited to SHBG, free hormone fractions, and adiposity/inflammation markers.

## Key Differences

| Domain | Men | Women |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Metabolomic breadth | Broad (alanine, BCAAs, tyrosine, omega-3, glycerol, LDL/IDL, SFA) | Narrow (glycerol, omega-3, leucine, citrate, glutamine, glycine) |
| Glucose-insulin | Favourable (↓ fasting insulin, ↓ HOMA2-IR, ↑ QUICKI) | Weak/few correlations |
| Total testosterone | ↑ significant | No association |
| Bioavailable testosterone | ↑ significant | No association |
| SHBG | ↑ (+0.57/cup) | ↑ (+1.27/cup) — stronger in women |
| Free testosterone / FAI | ↓ significant | ↓ significant |

## Possible Mechanisms

1. **Baseline testosterone difference:** Men's ~15x higher testosterone provides more substrate for SHBG-mediated regulation
2. **Body fat distribution:** Men carry more visceral fat (more metabolically active); coffee's adiposity reduction may have larger metabolic impact in men
3. **Enzyme expression:** Sex differences in CYP1A2 (caffeine metabolism) and hepatic enzyme activity
4. **Hormonal regulation:** Women's oestradiol modulates SHBG differently than testosterone; menstrual cycle adds variability

## Clinical Significance

- Sex-specific dietary recommendations for coffee may be warranted
- The SHBG pathway appears protective in both sexes, but testosterone pathway is male-specific
- Women's weaker metabolic correlations don't imply no benefit — SHBG-mediated T2D protection operates in women
- Research should report sex-stratified results by default
