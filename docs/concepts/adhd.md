# ADHD (Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder)

## Definition

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition spanning cognitive, behavioral, and affective domains: (a) attentional processing and executive function, (b) response inhibition and impulsive behavior, and (c) overactive motor output. Diagnosis is made when these symptoms lie at the far end of the normative continuum and yield impairment in essential life functions.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

Like nearly all forms of psychopathology, the constituent behaviors are spectrum-related phenomena; dimensional accounts are usually superior to categorical models with respect to scientific validity.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Core Mechanisms

### Executive Function Deficits

The most replicated cognitive finding in ADHD is impaired executive function (EF) — including response inhibition, working memory, planning, and cognitive flexibility.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md] EF deficits are neither necessary nor sufficient for all cases, but they constitute a core pathway.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Multiple Neural Pathways

ADHD is not a single-deficit disorder. Multiple partially independent mechanisms contribute:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Executive dysfunction** | Inhibition, working memory, planning deficits |
| **Delay aversion** | Preference for immediate over delayed rewards |
| **Reaction time variability** | Inconsistent response speed (319+ studies meta-analyzed) |
| **Reward pathway dysfunction** | Dopamine reward pathway anomalies (Volkow et al. 2009, 2010) |
| **Default mode network** | Intrusion of task-negative networks during focused activity |
| **Cortical maturation delay** | Delayed cortical thinning, particularly prefrontal cortex (Shaw et al. 2007) |

These multiple pathways produce **equifinality** — different causal forces converging on parallel symptom displays — and **multifinality** — early markers not inevitably associated with later impairment.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Etiology

### Heritability

ADHD is among the most heritable psychiatric conditions:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

| Population | Heritability |
|-----------|-------------|
| Symptom dimensions (youth) | ~0.75 |
| Clinically diagnosed cases | ~0.90 |
| Adults (self-report) | Appears lower |
| Adults (cross-informant/clinical) | Nearly as high as youth |

Key caveats:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- High heritability does NOT mean single genes are responsible — combinations of many genes act in concert
- High heritability does NOT mean environments lack importance
- Shared genetic vulnerability exists across major psychiatric disorders (Cross-Disorder Group 2013)
- Heritabilities may not be uniform across socioeconomic spectra

### Risk Factors

| Factor | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| Low birth weight | Lower weight → higher ADHD risk; dose-response relationship |
| Prenatal alcohol | Binges → fetal alcohol syndrome; lesser amounts → ADHD symptoms |
| Prenatal nicotine | Complex gene × toxic interaction (Thapar et al. 2009) |
| Lead exposure | Implicated in ADHD symptoms and compromised IQ |
| Severe early deprivation | Romanian adoptee studies show ADHD-like patterns |
| Organophosphate pesticides | CHAMACOS study: exposure linked to attention problems |
| Prenatal acetaminophen | Long-term exposure linked to differential gene methylation in ADHD |

### Gene–Environment Interplay

ADHD is expressed via transactional patterns involving:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Gene–environment correlation** (rGE): genetic propensities shape the environments individuals experience
- **Gene–environment interaction** (G×E): genetic liability is potentiated by environmental factors
- **Epigenetic forces**: environmental exposures alter gene expression patterns (e.g., Gervin et al. 2017)

## Developmental Pathways

### Onset and Persistence

- Symptoms often emerge during preschool years^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- More often than not, ADHD extends into adulthood^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Longitudinal studies (Klein et al. 2012) show continuing impairment 33 years after childhood diagnosis^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Comorbidity

ADHD shows high comorbidity with:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

| Domain | Conditions |
|--------|-----------|
| **Externalizing** | ODD, conduct disorder, substance use |
| **Internalizing** | Anxiety, depression |
| **Learning** | Dyslexia, specific learning disorders |
| **Neurodevelopmental** | Autism spectrum, language disorders |

Comorbidity patterns differ by gender: boys show more externalizing comorbidities; girls show more internalizing.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Life-Span Impairments

| Life Stage | Impairments |
|-----------|------------|
| **Childhood** | Academic underachievement, social rejection, accidental injury |
| **Adolescence** | Delinquency, substance use, risky behavior, peer problems |
| **Adulthood** | Unemployment, substance abuse, risky driving, self-harm, relationship problems |

US costs: hundreds of billions of dollars annually (direct + indirect).^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Girls and Women with ADHD

Females are chronically underdiagnosed and understudied:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

| Aspect | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Referral bias** | Boys 3–4× more likely to be referred for treatment |
| **Symptom presentation** | Girls show more inattentive, fewer hyperactive symptoms |
| **Comorbidity** | More internalizing, eating pathology, self-harm |
| **Adult outcomes** | Elevated risk for suicide attempts, intimate partner victimization |
| **Parenting stress** | Mediates outcomes for girls with ADHD |

Key prospective studies (Hinshaw et al. 2002, 2006, 2012; Owens et al. 2017) follow girls with ADHD into early adulthood, showing continuing cross-domain impairment and elevated risk for self-injury and suicide attempts.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Surging US Prevalence

US diagnosed prevalence has risen 41% in the past decade; 11% of youth aged 4–17 now diagnosed.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md] Contributing factors:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

| Factor | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Increased recognition** | Greater awareness among clinicians and parents |
| **Policy changes** | ADHD diagnosis → eligibility for services |
| **NCLB accountability** | Unintended consequence: ADHD diagnoses increase in low-SES public schools |
| **Pharmaceutical advertising** | US + New Zealand only nations allowing direct-to-consumer drug ads |
| **Geographic variation** | Mirrors "small area variation" in healthcare (Wennberg & Gittelsohn 1973) |

Worldwide prevalence is consistently ~5–7% across nations with compulsory education, suggesting the underlying symptom domains reflect a neurobiological propensity that emerges in classroom settings.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| ADHD is a US-only phenomenon | Worldwide prevalence ~5–7%; exists across cultures |
| ADHD is just fidgety boys | Affects females, adults, all socioeconomic groups |
| ADHD is caused by bad parenting | Highly heritable (~0.75); parenting interacts with but doesn't cause ADHD |
| ADHD is overdiagnosed everywhere | US diagnosed prevalence outstrips true prevalence, but many cases are also missed (especially girls) |
| Stimulants are "smart pills" | Cognitive enhancement in healthy people is modest and inconsistent |
| ADHD is outgrown | Often persists into adulthood with different symptom presentation |
| All-biological or all-cultural | Reductionist — both interact transactionally |
| Medication alone is sufficient | Multimodal treatment needed for functional outcomes |

## Treatment

### Evidence-Based Interventions

| Treatment | Evidence | Target |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Stimulant medication** | 70–80% acute response rate; first-line | Core symptom reduction |
| **Behavioral parent training** | Strong evidence; meta-analysis (Fabiano et al. 2009) | Parenting practices, home behavior |
| **Classroom management** | Strong evidence | Academic functioning, school behavior |
| **CBT for adult ADHD** | Evidence-based (Solanto 2011) | Executive dysfunction, coping skills |
| **Multimodal (med + behavioral)** | MTA study: optimal for promoting competencies | Functional outcomes |

### MTA Study

The Multimodal Treatment Study of ADHD (MTA) is the landmark longitudinal RCT. Key findings:^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- 14-month trial: carefully monitored medication + behavioral intervention superior for promoting competencies
- Long-term follow-up (8+ years): initial benefits diminish without continued treatment
- Medication alone insufficient for functional outcomes (academic, social, emotional)
- Adolescent substance use related more to childhood conduct than to medication exposure

### Insufficient Evidence

| Treatment | Status |
|-----------|--------|
| **Neurofeedback** | Triple-blind RCT showed no superiority over sham (Schönenberg et al. 2017) |
| **Working memory training** | Limited transfer; gains don't generalize to ADHD symptoms |
| **Physical exercise** | Promising but preliminary |
| **Dietary restrictions** | Meta-analysis shows small effects for food color additives in subset |

## Open Questions

1. Specific genes contributing to ADHD vulnerability remain elusive^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
2. Gene–environment interplays relevant to particular symptoms and impairments largely unknown^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
3. Heterogeneity in symptom presentations, mechanisms, and outcomes needs untangling^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
4. Manifestations in females and adults remain less well identified^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
5. Precision intervention — matching treatments to individual profiles — urgently needed^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Why It Matters

ADHD is one of the most prevalent neurodevelopmental conditions worldwide, with massive personal, familial, and economic costs. Understanding it requires multiple levels of analysis — spanning genes, neurotransmission, brain pathways, individual skill levels, family socialization, peer relationships, and educational and cultural forces — which must be integrated to surpass reductionist accounts, reduce stigma, and maximize the impact of prevention and intervention efforts.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
