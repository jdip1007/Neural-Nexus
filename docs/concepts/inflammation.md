# Inflammation

Inflammation is a complex biological response to harmful stimuli such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants. It is a protective mechanism involving the immune system, blood vessels, and molecular mediators. While acute inflammation is a normal and beneficial healing response, chronic inflammation is a pathological process that underlies many diseases including [[metabolic-disorder]]s, [[neurodegenerative-disease]]s, and autoimmune conditions.

## Types of Inflammation

### Acute Inflammation

**Characteristics:**
- Rapid onset (minutes to hours)
- Short duration (days to weeks)
- Primarily beneficial: eliminates pathogens, initiates healing
- Classic signs: redness, heat, swelling, pain, loss of function

**Mechanism:**
1. Vascular dilation (increased blood flow)
2. Increased vascular permeability (fluid leakage)
3. Leukocyte recruitment (neutrophils, monocytes)
4. Phagocytosis of pathogens/debris
5. Resolution and tissue repair

### Chronic Inflammation

**Characteristics:**
- Prolonged duration (weeks to years)
- Often maladaptive
- Tissue damage and fibrosis
- Involves different immune cell populations

**Causes:**
- Persistent infections
- Autoimmune reactions
- Metabolic overload (obesity, insulin resistance)
- Environmental toxins
- Persistent tissue damage

## Cellular Components

### Innate Immune Cells

**Neutrophils:**
- First responders to acute inflammation
- Phagocytose bacteria and debris
- Release reactive oxygen species and proteases
- Short-lived, die at site of inflammation

**Monocytes/Macrophages:**
- Circulate as monocytes, differentiate into macrophages in tissues
- Phagocytose pathogens and dead cells
- Secrete cytokines and growth factors
- Bridge innate and adaptive immunity

**Mast cells:**
- Release histamine and other mediators
- Important in allergic inflammation
- Located near blood vessels

**Dendritic cells:**
- Antigen-presenting cells
- Activate adaptive immune responses
- Bridge innate and adaptive immunity

### Adaptive Immune Cells

**T cells:**
- CD4+ helper T cells: coordinate immune responses
- CD8+ cytotoxic T cells: kill infected cells
- Regulatory T cells: suppress excessive inflammation
- Th17 cells: promote inflammation

**B cells:**
- Produce antibodies
- Differentiate into plasma cells
- Involved in humoral immunity

## Molecular Mediators

### Cytokines

**Pro-inflammatory cytokines:**
- **TNF-α**: Tumor necrosis factor-alpha; induces fever, promotes inflammation
- **IL-1β**: Interleukin-1 beta; activates immune cells, induces acute phase response
- **IL-6**: Interleukin-6; promotes B cell differentiation, acute phase protein production
- **IL-17**: Produced by Th17 cells; promotes neutrophil recruitment

**Anti-inflammatory cytokines:**
- **IL-10**: Suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine production
- **TGF-β**: Transforming growth factor-beta; promotes tissue repair, regulates immune response
- **IL-4**: Shifts immune response toward anti-inflammatory profile

### Chemokines

**Function:**
- Attract immune cells to sites of inflammation
- Guide leukocyte migration
- Specific for different cell types

**Examples:**
- IL-8 (CXCL8): attracts neutrophils
- MCP-1 (CCL2): attracts monocytes
- RANTES (CCL5): attracts T cells, eosinophils, basophils

### Lipid Mediators

**Prostaglandins:**
- Derived from arachidonic acid
- PGE2: vasodilation, pain, fever
- Important in acute inflammation

**Leukotrienes:**
- Also from arachidonic acid
- LTB4: neutrophil chemoattractant
- LTC4, LTD4: increase vascular permeability, bronchoconstriction

### Reactive Oxygen and Nitrogen Species

**ROS:**
- Superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hydroxyl radical
- Produced by phagocytes to kill pathogens
- Can cause tissue damage if excessive

**RNS:**
- Nitric oxide (NO)
- Produced by endothelial cells and macrophages
- Vasodilation, antimicrobial activity

## Inflammation in Metabolic Disorders

### Obesity-Induced Inflammation

**Adipose tissue inflammation:**
- Hypertrophic adipocytes secrete pro-inflammatory cytokines
- Macrophage infiltration into adipose tissue
- Chronic low-grade systemic inflammation

**Visceral fat:**
- More inflammatory than subcutaneous fat
- Higher macrophage content
- Greater cytokine production

**Systemic effects:**
- Elevated circulating TNF-α, IL-6, CRP
- Insulin resistance
- Endothelial dysfunction
- Promotion of atherosclerosis

### Insulin Resistance

**Mechanisms:**
- Cytokines activate inflammatory kinases (JNK, IKKβ)
- Serine phosphorylation of insulin receptor substrate impairs signaling
- Reduced glucose uptake
- Hyperinsulinemia

**Consequences:**
- Type 2 diabetes
- Metabolic syndrome
- Cardiovascular disease

## Inflammation in the Brain

### Neuroinflammation

**Unique aspects:**
- [[Blood-brain-barrier]] normally limits peripheral immune cell entry
- Resident immune cells: microglia, astrocytes
- Specialized immune privilege

**See:** [[neuroinflammation]] for detailed discussion

### Hypothalamic Inflammation

**Role in obesity:**
- Early response to high-fat diet
- Microglial activation and gliosis
- Disruption of energy balance regulation
- Contribution to leptin and insulin resistance

**Mechanisms:**
- Cytokine signaling disrupts hypothalamic neurons
- Glial activation amplifies inflammatory response
- Resetting of defended adiposity set-point

## Chronic Inflammation and Disease

### Cardiovascular Disease

**Atherosclerosis:**
- Chronic inflammatory disease of arteries
- Macrophage infiltration into vessel walls
- Cytokine-driven plaque formation and instability
- Inflammatory markers predict cardiovascular events

### Neurodegenerative Diseases

**Alzheimer's disease:**
- Microglial activation and cytokine production
- Chronic inflammation accelerates amyloid and tau pathology
- [[Blood-brain-barrier]] breakdown allows peripheral immune infiltration

**Parkinson's disease:**
- Microglial activation in substantia nigra
- Inflammatory cytokines contribute to dopaminergic neuron loss

### Autoimmune Diseases

**Multiple sclerosis:**
- Immune attack on myelin
- T cell and B cell involvement
- Chronic inflammation in CNS

**Rheumatoid arthritis:**
- Synovial inflammation
- Cytokine-driven joint destruction
- Systemic inflammatory effects

### Cancer

**Inflammation and carcinogenesis:**
- Chronic inflammation creates mutagenic environment
- ROS cause DNA damage
- Cytokines promote cell proliferation
- Angiogenesis

## Biomarkers of Inflammation

### Acute Phase Proteins

**C-reactive protein (CRP):**
- Produced by liver in response to IL-6
- General marker of inflammation
- Elevated in obesity, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease

**Fibrinogen:**
- Coagulation factor, acute phase reactant
- Elevated in inflammation

**Serum amyloid A:**
- Early acute phase protein
- Sensitive inflammation marker

### Cytokines

**Pro-inflammatory:**
- TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17
- Measured in serum or tissue

**Anti-inflammatory:**
- IL-10, TGF-β

### Cellular Markers

**Complete blood count:**
- Leukocytosis (elevated white blood cells)
- Neutrophilia (acute inflammation)
- Lymphocytosis (chronic inflammation)

**Flow cytometry:**
- Activation markers on immune cells
- T cell subset analysis

## Therapeutic Approaches

### Anti-Inflammatory Drugs

**NSAIDs:**
- Inhibit cyclooxygenase (COX)
- Reduce prostaglandin production
- Used for pain, fever, inflammation

**Corticosteroids:**
- Potent anti-inflammatory
- Inhibit multiple inflammatory pathways
- Side effects limit long-term use

### Biologics

**Cytokine inhibitors:**
- Anti-TNF-α agents (infliximab, adalimumab)
- IL-1 receptor antagonist (anakinra)
- IL-6 receptor blockers (tocilizumab)

**Used for:**
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Inflammatory bowel disease
- Psoriasis

### Lifestyle Interventions

**Diet:**
- Anti-inflammatory diets (Mediterranean, DASH)
- Omega-3 fatty acids
- Antioxidant-rich foods

**Exercise:**
- Regular physical activity reduces chronic inflammation
- Increases anti-inflammatory myokines

**Weight loss:**
- Reduces adipose tissue inflammation
- Improves insulin sensitivity
- Lowers systemic inflammatory markers

**Stress reduction:**
- Chronic stress promotes inflammation
- Mindfulness, meditation reduce inflammatory markers

## Research Advances

### Single-Cell Technologies

**Single-cell RNA sequencing:**
- Reveals heterogeneity of immune cell populations
- Identifies new cell types and states
- Characterizes inflammatory responses at cellular resolution

### Spatial Transcriptomics

**Mapping inflammation in tissue:**
- Location-specific gene expression
- Understanding microenvironment interactions
- Identifying therapeutic targets

### Immune Modulation

**Targeted therapies:**
- Cell-specific immunomodulation
- Precision anti-inflammatory approaches
- Minimal side effects
