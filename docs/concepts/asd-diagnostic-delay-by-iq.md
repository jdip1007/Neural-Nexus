# ASD Diagnostic Delay by IQ Level

**Definition:** The pattern where higher-IQ individuals with ASD receive their diagnosis significantly later (often adulthood) compared to lower-IQ individuals who are typically diagnosed in early childhood.

## Mechanism

Wolff et al. (2022) observed that in their clinical sample:
- Only **one-third** of ASD individuals were diagnosed before age 10
- Another **one-third** were diagnosed after age 20

Higher IQ enables compensatory strategies that mask ASD symptoms:
- **Social masking:** Learning explicit social rules to replace intuitive social understanding
- **Cognitive compensation:** Using intellectual capacity to "compute" social responses that neurotypical individuals process automatically
- **Self-monitoring:** High cognitive effort to suppress autistic behaviors in social settings

These strategies delay detection but come at significant cost: chronic stress, burnout, and mental health comorbidities.

## Dysfunction in Support Context

Late-diagnosed individuals face:
- No access to childhood educational supports and accommodations
- Identity disruption upon receiving diagnosis in adulthood
- Accumulated trauma from years of undiagnosed social failure
- Mental health comorbidities (anxiety, depression) that may predate and complicate ASD diagnosis
- Lack of age-appropriate services (most ASD support targets children)

## Clinical Significance

- Adult ASD diagnosis requires different assessment approaches than child diagnosis
- High-IQ individuals may present with subtle social difficulties that are real but not "severe enough" by child-centric criteria
- Diagnostic instruments may need different thresholds for compensated/high-IQ presentations
- Post-diagnostic support must address identity, trauma, and skill-building simultaneously
