# Inheritance Economics

## Definition

Inheritance economics is the study of how wealth is transferred between generations through inheritance, gifts, and family trusts, and the economic consequences of this intergenerational wealth transfer. It examines how inherited wealth affects economic opportunity, wealth concentration, and social mobility across generations.

## Mechanisms of Inheritance Transfer

### 1. Direct Inheritance
- **Estate transfers** through wills and probate processes
- **Life insurance payouts** providing lump-sum inheritances
- **Retirement account transfers** preserving wealth across generations
- **Property transfers** maintaining real estate ownership within families

### 2. Intergenerational Wealth Strategies
- **Trust funds** providing multi-generational wealth protection
- **Family businesses** transferring entrepreneurial capital
- **Educational endowments** ensuring family access to quality education
- **Healthcare assets** preserving family health and well-being

### 3. Indirect Inheritance Benefits
- **Social capital** inherited through family networks and connections
- **Educational advantages** through family emphasis on learning
- **Cultural capital** inherited through family values and traditions
- **Geographic advantages** through inherited property and location

### 4. Policy-Influenced Transfer
- **Tax-advantaged transfers** through estate planning strategies
- **Retirement account rules** facilitating wealth preservation
- **Housing policies** benefiting existing homeowners
- **Interest rate policies** enriching asset owners ^[readings/infographics-show-boomers-inequality-2026.md]
