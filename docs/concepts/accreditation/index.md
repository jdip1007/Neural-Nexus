# Laboratory Accreditation

Laboratory accreditation is the formal recognition that a laboratory is competent to perform specific tests and examinations, following defined quality standards and technical requirements.

## Overview

Accreditation provides independent verification that a laboratory operates according to international standards, ensuring reliable test results and patient safety.

## Major Accreditation Bodies

### CLIA (Clinical Laboratory Improvement Amendments)
- US federal program regulating laboratory testing
- Applies to all laboratories testing human specimens for health assessment
- Operated by CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services)
- Focuses on ensuring test accuracy, reliability, and timeliness

### CAP (College of American Pathologists)
- Voluntary accreditation program
- Uses checklist-based inspections
- Recognized as deemed status under CLIA
- More frequent inspections than CLIA alone

### ISO 15189
- International standard for medical laboratory quality
- Used globally for accreditation
- Emphasizes quality management system and technical competence
- Often basis for national accreditation schemes

## Accreditation Requirements

### Quality Management System
- Documented policies and procedures
- Quality indicators and monitoring
- Internal audits and management reviews
- Corrective and preventive actions
- Document control and records management

### Personnel Requirements
- Qualified laboratory director
- Appropriate technical supervisors
- Trained and competent testing personnel
- Continuing education programs
- Competency assessment

### Facilities and Equipment
- Appropriate physical facilities
- Calibrated and maintained equipment
- Environmental monitoring
- Safety programs
- Information systems validation

### Pre-Analytic Phase
- Patient identification
- Specimen collection procedures
- Specimen transport and storage
- Test ordering and accessioning
- Sample adequacy criteria

### Analytic Phase
- Validated test methods
- Quality control programs
- Calibration verification
- Proficiency testing participation
- Method verification and validation

### Post-Analytic Phase
- Result verification and review
- Critical value reporting
- Reference intervals
- Result reporting and interpretation
- Consultation services

## Accreditation Process

1. **Application**
   - Submit application to accreditation body
   - Pay applicable fees
   - Review checklist requirements

2. **Preparation**
   - Conduct gap analysis
   - Implement required procedures
   - Train staff
   - Perform internal audits

3. **Documentation**
   - Prepare policy manual
   - Compile procedure manuals
   - Document quality system
   - Maintain records

4. **Inspection**
   - On-site inspection or desktop review
   - Interview with staff
   - Review of documents and records
   - Observation of testing procedures

5. **Corrective Actions**
   - Address deficiencies identified
   - Submit evidence of corrections
   - Respond to inspection findings

6. **Accreditation Decision**
   - Review of all submitted materials
   - Accreditation award or denial
   - Periodic surveillance inspections
   - Reaccreditation cycles

## Benefits of Accreditation

- Demonstrates commitment to quality
- Improves laboratory operations
- Enhances patient safety
- Supports insurance reimbursement
- Facilitates laboratory networking
- Enables international recognition
