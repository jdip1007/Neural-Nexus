# Neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's Disease

## Definition

Neuroinflammation in  refers to the activation of innate and adaptive immune cells in the brain, particularly microglia and astrocytes, in response to pathological changes including [amyloid-beta](concepts/amyloid-beta.md) plaques, [tau-pathology](concepts/tau-pathology.md), and neuronal damage. Once thought to be a secondary response, neuroinflammation is now recognized as a central driver of AD pathogenesis and a critical therapeutic target.^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Core Mechanisms

### Innate Immune System

#### Microglial Activation
- **Resident immune cells:** Microglia are brain's primary innate immune cells
- **Dual role:** Can be protective (phagocytosis of Aβ) or detrimental (chronic inflammation, synapse loss)
- **Receptor-mediated:** Triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2 (TREM2) signaling implicated in AD^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

**Mechanisms in AD:**
- Phagocytosis of Aβ plaques (required for anti-amyloid antibody efficacy)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Secretion of inflammatory mediators (cytokines, chemokines)
- Contribution to tau propagation via direct uptake and spread^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Synaptic pruning and elimination (can be excessive in AD)

#### Astrocyte Reactivity
- **Reactive astrogliosis:** Response to brain injury and pathology
- **Multifaceted contributions:**
  - Secretion of inflammatory mediators
  - Phagocytic clearance or failure thereof
  - Metabolic support to neurons (can be disrupted)
  - Direct uptake and spread of pathological tau species^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Adaptive Immune System
- **Historical view:** Brain considered "immune-privileged" with minimal adaptive immune involvement
- **Current understanding:** Recognition of adaptive immune system importance in AD^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **T cells:** Contribute to neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration
- **B cells:** May produce antibodies; potential therapeutic target

**Key insights:**
- Both innate AND adaptive immunity contribute to AD pathogenesis^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Opens new therapeutic avenues beyond amyloid and tau

## Inflammatory Pathways

#### Complement Cascade
- **Classical pathway activation:** C1q binds to synapses, marking them for elimination
- **Synapse loss:** Excessive complement-mediated pruning contributes to cognitive decline
- **Therapeutic targeting:** Complement inhibitors under investigation

#### Cytokine Signaling
- **Pro-inflammatory cytokines:** IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α elevated in AD brain and CSF
- **Anti-inflammatory cytokines:** IL-10, TGF-β may be dysregulated
- **Balance:** Shift toward pro-inflammatory state in AD

#### Pattern Recognition Receptors
- **Toll-like receptors (TLRs):** Recognize Aβ and tau aggregates
- **NLRP3 inflammasome:** Activated by Aβ, leads to IL-1β release
- **RAGE:** Receptor for advanced glycation end-products; mediates Aβ effects

## Biomarkers

### PET Imaging Biomarkers

| Tracer | Target | Status |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **TSPO** (e.g., ^18F-GE387) | Microglial activation | Widely used; rs6971 polymorphism affects binding^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| **MAO-B** (e.g., ^18F-SMBT-1, ^11C-deuterium-L-deprenyl) | Astrocyte reactivity | First-in-human evaluation for ^18F-SMBT-1^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| **COX-1/COX-2** | Inflammatory enzymes | Under development |

**TSPO PET insights:**
- Microglia measured by TSPO PET associated with AD pathology^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Mediates key steps in disease progression model^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- Brain inflammation co-localizes highly with tau in mild cognitive impairment^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Fluid Biomarkers

| Biomarker | Source | What It Reflects | Clinical Utility |
|-----------|--------|------------------|------------------|
| **GFAP** (glial fibrillary acidic protein) | Astrocytes | Early marker of amyloid pathology; neuroinflammation^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] | Predicts conversion; detects amyloid before tau |
| **sTREM2** (soluble TREM2) | Microglia | Microglial response; immune activation^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] | Increases after amyloid deposition and neuronal injury |
| **YKL-40** (CHI3L1) | Astrocytes/microglia | Neuroinflammation | Elevated in AD; correlates with disease severity |

**Biomarker timeline:**
- Plasma GFAP: Early marker of amyloid pathology, not tau^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- CSF sTREM2: Early increase associated with tau-related neurodegeneration, not amyloid^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Therapeutic Targeting

### Anti-Inflammatory Approaches

#### Steroidal Anti-Inflammatories
- **Historical trials:** NSAIDs, corticosteroids failed to show benefit
- **Timing issue:** May need earlier intervention (preclinical stage)

#### Immunomodulatory Therapies
- **Nasal anti-CD3:** Ameliorates disease in mouse model; modulates T cell responses^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Nasal foralumab:** Dampening of microglial activation in moderate AD dementia^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Low-dose IL-2:** Restores regulatory T-cell dysfunction in AD^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

#### TREM2-Targeted Approaches
- **AL002:** Anti-TREM2 antibody; Phase 2 trial in early AD (INVOKE-2)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Mechanism:** Enhances microglial phagocytic function, modulates inflammatory response

#### p38 MAPK Inhibition
- **Isoform-selective p38α inhibitors:** Rescue early entorhinal cortex dysfunctions in mouse models^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Rationale:** p38 MAPK pathway central to inflammatory signaling

### CAR-Based Immunotherapies
- **CAR macrophages:** Target and resorb amyloid plaques^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **CAR-T cells:** Engineered to target AD pathology^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **CAR astrocytes:** Target amyloid-beta pathology^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

### Combination Approaches
- **Anti-amyloid + anti-inflammatory:** Leverage amyloid removal to reduce inflammatory stimulus
- **Anti-tau + anti-inflammatory:** Target both pathologies and inflammatory response

## Current State

### Established Understanding
- **Central player:** Neuroinflammation is core component of AD pathogenesis, not secondary response^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Dual innate + adaptive:** Both immune systems contribute^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Validated biomarkers:** GFAP, sTREM2, TSPO PET enable monitoring
- **Therapeutic target:** Multiple approaches in clinical trials

### Emerging Insights
- **Timing matters:** Neuroinflammation may be protective early, detrimental late
- **Heterogeneity:** Different inflammatory states across disease stages and brain regions
- **Cell-type specificity:** Microglial vs astrocytic contributions differ

### Ongoing Challenges
- **Therapeutic window:** When to intervene for optimal benefit?
- **Target selection:** Which inflammatory pathways to enhance vs suppress?
- **Biomarker precision:** Need more specific markers of different inflammatory states
- **Patient heterogeneity:** Inflammatory profiles vary across individuals

## Why It Matters

Neuroinflammation represents a critical therapeutic target in AD:
- **Mechanistic importance:** Contributes to all major AD pathologies (amyloid, tau, neurodegeneration)^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Clinical evidence:** Anti-amyloid antibodies reduce neuroinflammation; this correlates with clinical benefit^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Early marker:** GFAP detects amyloid pathology before tau markers^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]
- **Novel approaches:** CAR-based therapies and immunomodulation expanding therapeutic arsenal^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md]

## Open Questions

1. **Protective vs detrimental:** When does neuroinflammation switch from helpful to harmful?

2. **Cell-type specificity:** What are the relative contributions of microglia vs astrocytes vs T cells?

3. **Optimal modulation:** Should we enhance or suppress specific inflammatory pathways?

4. **Timing of intervention:** When is the optimal window for anti-inflammatory therapies?

5. **Biomarker refinement:** Can we develop more specific markers of distinct inflammatory states?

6. **Combination strategies:** How to best combine anti-inflammatory with anti-amyloid/anti-tau approaches?

7. **Individual differences:** How do genetic factors (APOE, TREM2) influence inflammatory responses?

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| Neuroinflammation is just a secondary response | Central driver of AD pathogenesis, not secondary^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| Only innate immunity matters | Both innate and adaptive immune systems contribute^[raw/articles/recent-advances-alzheimers-disease-cell-review-2026.md] |
| Anti-inflammatories don't work in AD | Early trials failed; new approaches (immunomodulation, CAR therapy) showing promise |
| Inflammation is always bad | Can be protective early (phagocytosis); becomes detrimental when chronic |
| Microglia are the only players | Astrocytes and T cells also make major contributions |
| All inflammation is the same | Different inflammatory states across disease stages, brain regions, and individuals |
