# Calibration Verification

## Definition

Calibration verification is the process of confirming that a measurement system's current calibration remains valid across the reportable range. It assesses whether the relationship between analyte concentration and instrument signal established during calibration has remained stable, ensuring that patient results are traceable and accurate over time. In the United States, CLIA regulations (42 CFR §493.1255) require calibration verification at defined intervals and after major system changes.

## Key Points

- **Purpose:** Verify that the calibration is stable and that reported values remain accurate throughout the analytical measurement range (AMR).
- **Distinguished from recalibration:** Recalibration re-establishes the calibration curve; verification confirms the existing curve is still valid without changing it.
- **CLIA triggers:** Calibration verification is required every six months, after major service/replacement of critical components, when reagent lot changes affect performance, and after a defined set of QC failures.
- **Materials:** Uses materials with known values spanning low, mid, and high points of the reportable range. Acceptability criteria are based on the laboratory's established limits.
- **Documentation:** Results, dates, materials, lot numbers, and acceptability must be documented per [[clia-regulations]] and laboratory [[quality-control]] policy.
- Calibration verification is a component of ongoing [[method-performance]] monitoring rather than one-time validation.

## Typical Workflow

1. Select verification materials at low, mid, and high AMR points.
2. Analyze materials using the current calibration.
3. Compare results to target values and established acceptability limits.
4. If all acceptable → calibration verified; document.
5. If any unacceptable → investigate, recalibrate, and repeat verification.
