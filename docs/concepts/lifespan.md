# Lifespan

## Definition

Lifespan (longevity) refers to the duration of life of an organism, typically measured as the maximum or average lifespan observed within a species or population. In comparative physiology and metabolic scaling research, lifespan scales allometrically with body mass and inversely with metabolic rate: larger animals tend to live longer and have slower metabolisms, smaller hearts, and lower heart rates. This relationship is central to [metabolic-scaling](concepts/metabolic-scaling.md) theory and underlies phenomena such as the [one-billion-heartbeats-phenomenon](concepts/one-billion-heartbeats-phenomenon.md) and [kleibers-law](concepts/kleibers-law.md).

## Key Points

- **Allometric scaling:** Maximum lifespan (M) scales approximately as M ∝ (body mass)^0.2 to ^0.25 across mammals, mirroring the exponent in [kleibers-law](concepts/kleibers-law.md) for metabolic rate.
- **Rate-of-living theory:** Total metabolic energy per gram per lifetime is roughly constant across species, linking lifespan inversely to mass-specific metabolic rate.
- **Heart rate and the heartbeat budget:** Smaller animals have faster heart rates and shorter lives, yet the total number of heartbeats over a lifetime is remarkably conserved — the [one-billion-heartbeats-phenomenon](concepts/one-billion-heartbeats-phenomenon.md) (~1 billion beats in shrews; ~2.5 billion in humans).
- **Exceptions and modulation:** Birds and bats live longer than metabolic scaling predicts for their size; caloric restriction, genetic factors (e.g., IIS/FOXO pathways), and environmental conditions modulate lifespan.
- **Mechanistic basis:** Oxidative stress, telomere attrition, proteostasis, and mitochondrial efficiency connect metabolic rate to aging, though no single mechanism fully explains scaling.
- **Comparative framework:** Lifespan scaling is a cornerstone of [metabolic-scaling](concepts/metabolic-scaling.md) and comparative physiology, informing models from ecology to biomedical aging research.
