# Urban Scaling

**Urban scaling** extends biological scaling theory to cities, finding that urban metrics follow predictable power-law relationships with population size.

## Key Findings

Unlike biological organisms (which scale sublinearly at ¾), cities show two scaling regimes:

| Metric Type | Exponent | Examples |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| Infrastructure | ~0.8 (sublinear) | Roads, gas stations, electrical cables |
| Social/economic | ~1.15 (superlinear) | GDP, patents, wages, crime |
| Individual output | ~1.0 (linear) | Income per capita, housing cost |

## Theoretical Basis

[geoffrey-west](entities/geoffrey-west.md) and colleagues proposed that superlinear social scaling results from increasing interaction rates in denser populations, analogous to metabolic scaling in organisms but with different network properties.
