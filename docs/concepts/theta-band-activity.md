# Theta-Band Activity

## Definition

Theta-band activity refers to neural oscillations in the 6–9 Hz frequency range (in mice) as measured by electrocorticogram (ECoG) or electroencephalogram (EEG). Theta oscillations are prominent during both REM sleep and intermittently during NREM sleep, but play distinct roles in each state.

## Role in NREM Sleep

- Theta-band power fluctuations during NREM sleep are tightly coupled to [brain-blood-volume](concepts/brain-blood-volume.md) changes
- Theta-band activity **precedes** BBV changes by ~4–5 seconds
- This predictive relationship can be modeled by a FIR-BRF (finite-impulse-response brain-blood-volume response function)
- Suggests a homeostatic mechanism that adjusts energy delivery to match neuronal demand

## Role in REM Sleep

- REM sleep is characterized by persistently high theta-band power
- Unlike NREM, theta-band power during REM does not show the same predictive relationship with BBV
- The strong, sustained theta activity during REM may contribute to the high metabolic demand
