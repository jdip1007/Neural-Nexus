# Coffee and SHBG

**Definition:** The positive association between habitual coffee intake and sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) observed in both men and women, which may mediate coffee's protective cardiometabolic effects.

## Mechanism

SHBG is a glycoprotein that binds sex hormones (testosterone, oestradiol) and regulates their bioavailability. Higher SHBG means:
- Less free (biologically active) testosterone circulating
- Reduced androgenic stimulation of adipose tissue
- Improved insulin sensitivity (SHBG is inversely associated with insulin resistance)

Coffee may increase SHBG through:
1. **Reduced adiposity:** Lower visceral fat → lower insulin → higher SHBG production (hepatic SHBG synthesis is suppressed by insulin)
2. **Direct hepatic effects:** Coffee bioactives (chlorogenic acids, caffeine) may directly influence hepatic SHBG synthesis
3. **Improved insulin sensitivity:** Lower HOMA-IR → less SHBG suppression

## Evidence

- **NFBC1966:** Men β = +0.57 nmol/L per cup/day (p < 0.001); Women β = +1.27 nmol/L per cup/day (p = 0.015)
- **Tromsø Study / NHANES III:** SHBG positively associated with coffee intake in both cohorts
- **Women's Health Study:** SHBG increase partly mediated lower T2D risk among high coffee consumers
- **Nurses' Health Study:** Higher caffeine/coffee linked to higher SHBG in postmenopausal women

## Clinical Significance

- SHBG is an independent predictor of T2D risk in both sexes
- Low SHBG associated with coronary artery disease (postmenopausal women) and peripheral artery disease (older individuals)
- The coffee-SHBG association may be the primary hormonal pathway through which coffee protects against MetS and T2D
- SHBG's role as mediator is more consistent across studies than testosterone associations
