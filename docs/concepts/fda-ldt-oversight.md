# FDA LDT Oversight

## Definition

FDA oversight of Laboratory-Developed Tests (LDTs) refers to the regulatory framework under which the U.S. Food and Drug Administration regulates in vitro diagnostic tests designed, manufactured, and used within a single laboratory. Historically, LDTs were regulated primarily under [clia-regulations](concepts/accreditation/clia-regulations.md) through laboratory accreditation and validation requirements, with FDA exercising enforcement discretion. Recent rulemaking (2024–2025) has progressively brought LDTs under the FDA's medical device framework, requiring premarket review, quality system regulation, and labeling for most new and existing LDTs in phases.

## Key Points

- **Dual oversight context:** LDTs have long been subject to CLIA validation and [laboratory-validation](concepts/accreditation/laboratory-validation.md) requirements; FDA oversight adds device-level controls (premarket submission, QSR, registration/listing).
- **Phased implementation:** The 2024 final rule phases in requirements over multiple years, with staged enforcement discretion policies for different test categories and risk levels.
- **Risk-based premarket pathways:** High-risk LDTs (e.g., companion diagnostics) typically require PMA; moderate-risk tests may use 510(k); the applicable pathway depends on intended use and risk classification.
- **Exemptions and carve-outs:** Certain categories (e.g., tests for unmet needs, forensic use, some public health emergencies) may receive tailored enforcement policies or exemptions.
- **Interaction with CLIA:** FDA oversight complements rather than replaces CLIA laboratory certification and quality requirements; both frameworks may apply simultaneously.
- **Impact on [laboratory-developed-tests](concepts/accreditation/laboratory-developed-tests.md) practices:** Laboratories must establish device-quality systems, historical test inventories, and compliance documentation alongside existing CLIA quality programs.
