# Coffee and Testosterone

**Definition:** The sex-specific association between habitual coffee intake and circulating androgen markers, with men showing a broader androgen-related hormonal profile (higher total testosterone, bioavailable testosterone, SHBG; lower free testosterone and FAI) while women show only SHBG and free hormone fraction associations.

## Mechanism

The apparent paradox — higher total testosterone but lower free testosterone with more coffee — resolves through SHBG:

1. **Coffee ↑ SHBG:** More sex hormone-binding globulin binds more testosterone
2. **Bound testosterone ↑:** Total testosterone increases (bound + free)
3. **Free testosterone ↓:** The fraction of unbound (biologically active) testosterone decreases because SHBG sequesters it
4. **FAI ↓:** Free androgen index (total T / SHBG) decreases

This pattern is **hormonally favourable**: low total testosterone and low SHBG are independent predictors of MetS and T2D. Higher SHBG is protective regardless of total testosterone level.

## Sex Differences

| Marker | Men | Women |
|--------|-----|-------|
| Total testosterone | ↑ (+0.29 nmol/L/cup/day) | No association |
| Bioavailable testosterone | ↑ (+0.08 nmol/L/cup/day) | No association |
| SHBG | ↑ (+0.57 nmol/L/cup/day) | ↑ (+1.27 nmol/L/cup/day) |
| Free testosterone | ↓ (−0.01 nmol/L/cup/day) | ↓ (−0.01 nmol/L/cup/day) |
| FAI | ↓ (−0.36/cup/day) | ↓ (−0.32/cup/day) |

Women's limited testosterone response likely reflects their much lower baseline testosterone and different endocrine regulation.

## Clinical Significance

- The pattern (↑ total T + ↑ SHBG + ↓ FAI) maps onto a metabolically favourable hormonal profile
- SHBG mediates coffee's protective association with T2D in women (Women's Health Study)
- Acute caffeine effects (exercise studies: ↑ testosterone 20%+) are transient; habitual intake effects are smaller but sustained
- Population-level pattern, not clinically meaningful individual change
