# Coffee and Body Composition

**Definition:** The observation that higher habitual coffee intake is associated with a leaner body composition profile — lower body fat percentage, visceral fat, and higher skeletal muscle mass — despite comparable BMI across consumption groups.

## Mechanism

Multiple pathways contribute:

1. **Adenosine receptor antagonism:** Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors → sympathetic nervous system activation → increased resting metabolic rate and thermogenic lipolysis
2. **Enhanced fat oxidation:** Meta-analyses show pre-exercise caffeine enhances fat oxidation during aerobic exercise in both fasting and fed states
3. **Appetite modulation:** Coffee modulates satiety hormones (serotonin, ghrelin); dietary fiber from spent coffee grounds increases satiety and reduces ad libitum energy intake
4. **Reduced SCFA absorption:** Chronic coffee intake may reduce short-chain fatty acid absorption, contributing to weight regulation

## Evidence

- **NFBC1966 (n=2,264):** Higher coffee groups showed dose-related trends: lower BIA body fat %, fat mass, visceral fat area; higher skeletal muscle mass. BMI was similar across groups.
- **Mendelian randomization:** Genetically predicted higher plasma caffeine → lower BMI, lower whole-body fat mass, reduced T2D risk
- **RCT evidence:** 24-week RCT showed modest but significant fat mass reduction with daily caffeinated coffee; pilot RCT showed coffee fiber increases satiety

## Clinical Significance

- BMI alone misses the coffee-body composition association — body fat % and visceral fat are more informative
- The leaner profile may partly mediate coffee's protective cardiometabolic effects
- BIA-derived estimates are indirect; findings should be interpreted cautiously
