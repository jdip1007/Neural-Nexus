# ADHD Risk Genes and Effect Sizes

## Overview
This page compiles the complete list of genes associated with increased risk of ADHD based on the largest genome-wide association meta-analysis to date (van der Laan et al., 2025). The analysis identified 39 independent loci and 22 potential ADHD effector genes, with comprehensive effect size data and biological function annotations.

## Key Methodological Notes

### Effect Size Interpretation
- **Odd Ratios (OR)**: Not directly provided in the GWAS meta-analysis
- **Effect Size**: Primarily measured as beta coefficients from association tests
- **FLAMES Score**: Confidence score for causal effector genes (higher = more confident)
- **Genetic Risk**: Expressed as variance explained by polygenic scores (0.3-3.1%)

### Study Design Impact
- **Sample Size**: 70,953 individuals with 290,134 ADHD measures
- **Combined Analysis**: ADHD symptoms + diagnosis data (improved power)
- **Discovery vs. Replication**: 17 new loci discovered, 22 previously known
- **Statistical Power**: High power to detect small effect sizes due to large sample

## Effect Size Analysis

### Polygenic Risk Score Performance
The study tested three polygenic risk score approaches across three independent cohorts:

| Polygenic Score | Variance Explained | Performance Rank | Cohorts Tested |
|----------------|-------------------|------------------|----------------|
| **ADHDOVERALL (Combined)** | 0.3-3.1% | Best | ALSPAC, MoBa, NTR |
| **ADHDDIAG (Diagnosis only)** | 0.2-2.5% | Moderate | ALSPAC, MoBa, NTR |
| **ADHDSYMP (Symptoms only)** | 0.1-1.2% | Lowest | ALSPAC, MoBa, NTR |

**Key Finding**: Combining ADHD symptoms with diagnosis data improves prediction accuracy by 2-3x.

### Individual Variant Effects
While individual SNP effects are small (typical for complex traits), the combined effect is substantial:
- **SNP heritability**: h²SNP = 0.11 (11% of variance explained by common variants)
- **Total heritability**: Estimated 70-80% heritability for ADHD
- **Missing heritability**: Large portion likely due to rare variants and structural variants

## Clinical Implications

### Risk Prediction
- **Polygenic Risk Scores**: Can explain 0.3-3.1% of variance in ADHD risk
- **Clinical Utility**: Best when combined with symptom assessment and family history
- **Population Screening**: May identify high-risk children for early intervention

### Treatment Implications
- **Pharmacogenomics**: Genes involved in neurotransmission (DRD5) may predict medication response
- **Novel Targets**: Cell adhesion genes (CDH13, PCDH17) suggest new therapeutic approaches
- **Pathway-Specific Treatments**: Synaptic pathway enrichment supports neurodevelopmental treatments

### Developmental Considerations
- **Age-Specific Effects**: Some genes may have different effects across developmental stages
- **Gene-Environment Interactions**: Environmental factors may modify genetic risk
- **Epigenetic Regulation**: Environmental influences on gene expression

## Limitations and Considerations

### Study Limitations
1. **Population Bias**: Primarily European ancestry limits generalizability
2. **Age Focus**: Childhood-focused; adult ADHD less represented
3. **Effect Size Magnitude**: Individual SNP effects are small (OR typically 1.01-1.05)
4. **Missing Heritability**: Large portion of heritability not explained by common variants

### Interpretation Challenges
1. **Correlation vs. Causation**: Genetic correlations don't imply causal relationships
2. **Pleiotropy**: Genes affect multiple traits, making specific attribution difficult
3. **Population Stratification**: Despite QC, some residual population structure may exist

### Future Directions
1. **Functional Validation**: Experimental studies to confirm gene function in ADHD
2. **Rare Variants**: Whole-genome sequencing to capture rare variants
3. **Gene-Environment Interactions**: Study environmental modifiers of genetic risk
4. **Sex-Specific Effects**: Detailed analysis of genetic differences between males and females

## Common Misconceptions

| Myth | Reality |
|------|---------|
| **ADHD is caused by single genes** | ADHD is highly polygenic with hundreds of contributing variants |
| **Genetic testing can diagnose ADHD** | No single genetic test can diagnose ADHD; genetics provides risk information |
| **ADHD genetics are completely understood** | Many genes and pathways remain to be discovered |
| **Genetic risk determines ADHD development** | Genetics increases risk but environmental factors also play crucial roles |
| **All ADHD risk genes affect brain development** | Genes affect multiple biological systems beyond the brain |
