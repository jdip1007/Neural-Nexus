# ADHD: Controversy, Developmental Mechanisms, and Multiple Levels of Analysis

**Author:** Stephen P. Hinshaw (UC Berkeley / UCSF)
**Journal:** Annual Review of Clinical Psychology, Vol. 14, 2018, pp. 291–316
**DOI:** [10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-050817-084917](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-050817-084917)

## TL;DR

Comprehensive review integrating ADHD's neurobiological underpinnings with contextual factors. ADHD is highly heritable (~0.75) but expressed through transactional gene–environment interplay. Core controversies (overdiagnosis, stimulant use, cultural variation) are addressed with evidence. Girls/women are underdiagnosed. Multimodal treatment (medication + behavioral) is optimal.^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Key Points

### Facts and Controversy
- ADHD comprises two behavioral dimensions: inattentive/disorganized and hyperactive/impulsive^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Worldwide prevalence ~5–7% of youth; cross-national variation driven by methodological differences (diagnostic system, informant type)^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- US diagnosed prevalence has risen 41% in a decade; 11% of youth aged 4–17 now diagnosed^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- ADHD is not a US-only phenomenon — exists globally with compulsory education as a triggering social factor^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Core impairments: academic underachievement, emotion dysregulation, social ineptness, accidental injury, substance use, risky behaviors, employment problems^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- US costs measurable in hundreds of billions of dollars annually (direct + indirect)^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Etiology and Mechanisms
- **Heritability:** ~0.75 for symptom dimensions; ~0.90 for clinically diagnosed cases^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Adult heritability appears lower due to reliance on self-report; cross-informant ratings yield figures nearly as high as youth^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Shared genetic vulnerability across major psychiatric disorders (Cross-Disorder Group, 2013)^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Risk factors:** low birth weight, prenatal toxin exposure (alcohol, possibly nicotine), childhood lead exposure, severe early deprivation^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Gene–environment interplay:** gene–environment correlation and interaction potentiate genetic liability^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Epigenetics:** prenatal acetaminophen exposure linked to differential gene methylation in ADHD individuals^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Mechanisms:** executive function deficits (inhibition, working memory, planning), delay aversion, reaction time variability, reward pathway dysfunction (dopamine), default mode network anomalies, cortical maturation delay^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Multiple neural pathways → equifinality (different causal paths to same symptom display)^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Developmental Pathways and Comorbidity
- ADHD is a neurodevelopmental disorder with preschool onset, often persisting into adulthood^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- High comorbidity with oppositional defiant disorder, conduct disorder, anxiety, depression, learning disorders^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Comorbidity patterns differ by gender: boys more externalizing, girls more internalizing^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Adult outcomes: substance use, risky driving, unemployment, relationship problems, self-harm^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Girls and Women with ADHD
- Females chronically underdiagnosed and understudied^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Girls show more inattentive than hyperactive symptoms; fewer externalizing comorbidities^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Girls with ADHD face elevated risk for: internalizing disorders, eating pathology, self-harm, suicide attempts, intimate partner victimization^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Referral bias: boys with ADHD are 3–4× more likely to be referred for treatment^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Parenting stress mediates outcomes for girls with ADHD^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Surging US Prevalence
- Diagnosed prevalence far outstrips true prevalence^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Contributing factors: increased recognition, policy changes (ADHD diagnosis → services), pharmaceutical advertising^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Unintended consequence of No Child Left Behind (NCLB):** accountability legislation linked to increased ADHD diagnoses in low-SES public school children^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Geographic variation within US mirrors "small area variation" in healthcare (Wennberg & Gittelsohn 1973)^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Direct-to-consumer pharmaceutical advertising (US + New Zealand only) fuels diagnosis expansion^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

### Treatment Strategies
- **Medication (stimulants):** first-line for core symptom reduction; 70–80% acute response rate^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Behavioral/psychosocial:** parent training, classroom management, social skills training, cognitive-behavioral therapy^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **MTA study:** multimodal treatment (medication + behavioral) superior for promoting competencies, not just symptom reduction^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- Long-term benefits diminish without continued treatment; medication alone insufficient for functional outcomes^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Neurofeedback:** mixed evidence; well-controlled trials show no superiority over sham^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Working memory training:** limited transfer effects; gains don't generalize to ADHD symptoms^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Physical exercise:** promising but preliminary evidence^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]
- **Adult ADHD:** CBT targeting executive dysfunction is evidence-based^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## Key Quotes

> "ADHD far transcends its stereotypic presentation as problems of fidgeting and distractibility in middle-class, elementary school–aged boys who are parented permissively and who contend with stifling classrooms."^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

> "All-biological or all-cultural perspectives are therefore reductionist and short-sighted."^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

> "A comprehensive understanding of ADHD mandates multiple levels of analysis—spanning genes, neurotransmission, brain pathways, individual skill levels, family socialization, peer relationships, and educational and cultural forces."^[raw/articles/hinshaw-2018-adhd-review.md]

## References

### Key Points Summary (from review)

1. ADHD is highly heritable but not genetically deterministic — transactional gene–environment interplay is essential
2. Multiple neural mechanisms (executive function, delay aversion, reward processing, cortical maturation delay) contribute
3. ADHD in girls/women is underdiagnosed, with distinct impairment patterns (internalizing, self-harm, eating pathology)
4. US prevalence surge driven by recognition, policy (NCLB), and pharmaceutical advertising
5. Multimodal treatment (medication + behavioral) is optimal for promoting competencies
6. Precision intervention and better translational science are urgently needed
