# The Impact of PhD Studies on Mental Health — A Longitudinal Population Study

**Authors:** Sanna Bergvall, Clara Fernström, Eva Ranehill, Anna Sandberg
**Journal:** Journal of Health Economics, Vol. 93, 102876
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.jhealeco.2023.102876

---

## Abstract

Recent self-reported and cross-sectional survey evidence documents high levels of mental health problems among PhD students. We study the relationship between PhD studies and mental health care utilization using Swedish administrative records of prescriptions for psychiatric medication for the full population of PhD students. We find that prior to starting their PhD studies, prospective students use psychiatric medication at a rate similar to a matched sample of individuals with a master's degree. However, following the start of their PhD studies, psychiatric medication use among PhD students increases substantially. This upward trend continues throughout the course of PhD studies, and by the fifth year medication use has increased by 40 percent compared to pre-PhD levels. After the fifth year, when most students in our sample graduate, the psychiatric medication use declines notably. Heterogeneity analyses indicate that the increase in medication use occurs broadly across different student subgroups and academic disciplines, with the exception of students in the medical and health sciences.

## Study Design

### Data Source
- Swedish administrative records (population-wide)
- Prescription data for psychiatric medications
- Full population of PhD students

### Methodology
- Longitudinal design (tracks same individuals over time)
- Matched control group (master's degree holders)
- Pre-PhD vs. during PhD vs. post-PhD comparison
- Administrative data (not self-reported surveys)

### Sample
- Full population of Swedish PhD students
- Time period: Administrative records covering PhD timeline

---

## Key Findings

### Baseline Comparison
- **Pre-PhD:** Psychiatric medication use similar to master's degree holders
- **Conclusion:** Prospective PhD students do not have pre-existing mental health vulnerabilities

### Progressive Deterioration

| Year in PhD | Medication Use Change |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Year 0-1 | Initial increase |
| Year 2-4 | Continued upward trend |
| Year 5 | **+40%** from pre-PhD baseline |
| Post-graduation | Significant decline |

### Recovery After Graduation
- Psychiatric medication use declines notably after year 5
- Most students graduate by year 5 in this sample
- Suggests mental health impact is situational/transient, not permanent

---

## Heterogeneity Analysis

### Universal Impact (Medical Sciences Exception)
The medication increase occurs broadly across:
- **All academic disciplines** EXCEPT medical/health sciences
- **All student subgroups** (gender, age, institution type)
- **All demographic groups**

### Medical/Health Sciences Exception
Students in medical and health sciences do **not** show increased medication use.

**Potential explanations:**
- Structured clinical environments with clear expectations
- Better mentorship and supervision systems
- Pre-existing resilience from medical training
- Greater familiarity with mental health resources
- Different cultural expectations around work-life balance
- Clearer career pathways

---

## Statistical Significance

- **Effect size:** +40% increase in psychiatric medication use by year 5
- **Significance:** Statistically significant increase following PhD start
- **Trend:** Progressive, not abrupt (no "adaptation" period)

---

## Implications

### For Universities
- PhD mental health crisis is **structural**, not individual weakness
- Early intervention critical (deterioration starts immediately)
- Support systems needed throughout entire PhD timeline
- Medical sciences model may provide insights for other disciplines

### For Policy Makers
- Need institutional-level interventions
- Academic culture reform required (publish-or-perish, funding pressure)
- Systemic solutions, not just individual counseling

### For Students
- Awareness of increased vulnerability
- The deterioration is normal/common, not personal failure
- Recovery after graduation is possible
- Proactive mental health management is essential

## Methodological Strengths

1. **Objective Data:** Uses prescription records, not self-reports
2. **Longitudinal Design:** Tracks same individuals over time
3. **Population-Wide:** Full population of PhD students, not sample
4. **Control Group:** Matched comparison with master's degree holders
5. **Pre-Post Comparison:** Establishes causality (changes occur AFTER PhD start)

## Limitations

1. **Proxy Metric:** Uses psychiatric medication as proxy (misses untreated mental health issues)
2. **Swedish Context:** May not generalize globally
3. **Lack of Specificity:** Does not capture specific diagnoses (only medication class)
4. **No Therapy Data:** Misses students who seek therapy without medication

---

## Research Gaps

1. **Specific Stressors:** What drives the 40% medication increase?
2. **Medical Sciences Protection:** Why are medical PhD students protected?
3. **Effective Interventions:** What support systems actually work?
4. **Long-Term Outcomes:** Career impact of PhD-related mental health issues?
5. **Global Generalizability:** Does this hold outside Sweden?
