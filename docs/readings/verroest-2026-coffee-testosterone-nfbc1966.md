# Coffee Intake, Testosterone, and Cardiometabolic Markers (NFBC1966)

**Authors:** Verroest L, Jokelainen J, Choudhary S, Walkowiak J, Karhu T, Palaniswamy S, Auvinen J, Järvelin M-R, Herzig K-H, Raza GS (2026)
**Journal:** European Journal of Nutrition, 65, 215
**DOI:** 10.1007/s00394-026-04038-z

## Executive Summary

- In 2,264 adults aged 46 (NFBC1966), higher coffee intake associated with leaner body composition despite similar BMI — lower body fat %, visceral fat, and higher skeletal muscle mass
- Coffee intake inversely correlated with circulating BCAAs in both sexes — a potentially novel finding relevant to insulin resistance and cardiometabolic risk
- In men, coffee independently associated with higher total testosterone (+0.29 nmol/L per cup/day), bioavailable testosterone, and SHBG, alongside lower free testosterone and FAI
- In women, hormonal associations limited to higher SHBG and lower free testosterone/FAI — no total testosterone association
- Cross-sectional design; causality not established

## Key Findings

1. **Leaner body composition:** Higher coffee groups had lower body fat % (p < 0.001), lower fat mass (p = 0.002), lower visceral fat area (p = 0.048), and higher skeletal muscle mass (p < 0.001) — despite comparable BMI across groups

2. **Inverse BCAA correlation:** Coffee intake inversely correlated with branched-chain amino acids (isoleucine, leucine, valine) in both men and women. Elevated BCAAs are established predictors of insulin resistance, T2D, and CVD

3. **Favourable glucose-insulin profile (men):** Higher coffee intake correlated with lower fasting insulin, lower HOMA2-IR, and higher insulin sensitivity (QUICKI) — only in men

4. **Sex-specific testosterone pattern (men):** Fully adjusted models: total testosterone β = +0.29 nmol/L per cup/day (p < 0.001), bioavailable testosterone β = +0.08 (p = 0.003), SHBG β = +0.57 (p < 0.001). Inverse: FAI β = −0.36 (p < 0.001), free testosterone β = −0.01 (p < 0.001)

5. **SHBG pathway (women):** Coffee positively associated with SHBG (β = +1.27, p = 0.015) and inversely with FAI (β = −0.32, p = 0.026) and free testosterone (β = −0.01, p = 0.015). No total/bioavailable testosterone associations

6. **Metabolic correlation breadth:** Men showed broader metabolomic correlations (alanine, tyrosine, omega-3, glycerol, LDL/IDL fractions) than women (narrower: glycerol, omega-3, leucine, citrate, glutamine, glycine)

7. **BP findings:** Systolic BP modestly higher in high coffee consumers (~1.6–3.6 mmHg), but measured after 12h fast/coffee abstention — unlikely acute caffeine effect

## Clinical Implications

- The concurrent increase in total testosterone + SHBG + decrease in FAI maps onto a hormonally favourable profile — both low total testosterone and low SHBG are independent predictors of MetS and T2D in men
- SHBG is an independent predictor of T2D risk in both sexes; the coffee-SHBG association may partly mediate coffee's protective effect against T2D
- Inverse BCAA association may represent a novel metabolic pathway linking coffee to reduced insulin resistance
- Cross-sectional — cannot establish causality or rule out reverse causation (healthier people drink more coffee)
