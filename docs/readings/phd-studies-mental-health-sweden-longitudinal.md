# The Impact of PhD Studies on Mental Health — A Longitudinal Population Study

**Authors:** Sanna Bergvall, Clara Fernström, Eva Ranehill, Anna Sandberg
**Institutions:** University of Gothenburg, Stockholm University, Lund University
**Study Type:** Longitudinal population study using Swedish administrative records

---

## Abstract

Recent self-reported and cross-sectional survey evidence documents high levels of mental health problems among PhD students. This study examines the relationship between PhD studies and mental health care utilization using Swedish administrative records of prescriptions for psychiatric medication for the full population of PhD students.

**Key Findings:**

1. **Pre-PhD Baseline**: Prior to starting their PhD studies, prospective students use psychiatric medication at a rate similar to a matched sample of individuals with a master's degree

2. **Post-PhD Increase**: Following the start of PhD studies, psychiatric medication use increases substantially

3. **Progressive Deterioration**: The upward trend continues throughout PhD studies. By the fifth year, medication use has increased by **40 percent** compared to pre-PhD levels

4. **Recovery After Graduation**: After the fifth year (when most students graduate), psychiatric medication use declines notably

5. **Widespread Impact**: The increase in medication use occurs broadly across different student subgroups and academic disciplines, with one exception

6. **Medical Sciences Exception**: Students in medical and health sciences do not show the same increase in medication use

---

## Study Design

- **Data Source**: Swedish administrative records
- **Population**: Full population of PhD students in Sweden
- **Metric**: Prescriptions for psychiatric medication (proxy for mental health care utilization)
- **Methodology**: Longitudinal design with matched control group (master's degree holders)
- **Comparison**: Pre-PhD vs. during PhD vs. post-PhD medication use

---

## Key Insights

### PhD Studies Have Causal Impact on Mental Health

The longitudinal design (tracking same individuals over time) provides stronger evidence of causality than cross-sectional surveys. The medication use increases **after** starting PhD studies, not before.

### Severity Escalates Over Time

- 40% increase in psychiatric medication use by year 5
- Progressive deterioration suggests cumulative stress
- No "adaptation" period — deterioration continues through entire PhD

### Recovery Is Possible

The decline in medication use after graduation suggests that the mental health impact is situational/transient, not permanent.

### Medical/Health Sciences Exception

Why might medical PhD students fare better? Potential explanations:
- More structured clinical environments
- Better mentorship and support systems
- Pre-existing resilience from medical training
- Different cultural expectations
- Greater familiarity with mental health resources

---

## Implications

### For Universities

- Need systematic mental health support systems
- Early intervention is critical (deterioration starts early)
- Support should continue throughout entire PhD timeline

### For Policy Makers

- PhD mental health crisis is structural, not individual weakness
- Requires institutional-level interventions
- Academic culture needs reform (publish-or-perish, funding pressure)

### For Students

- Awareness of increased vulnerability during PhD studies
- Proactive mental health management
- The deterioration is normal/common, not personal failure
- Recovery after graduation is possible

## Limitations

- Uses psychiatric medication as proxy (misses untreated mental health issues)
- Swedish context may not generalize globally
- Does not capture specific mental health diagnoses (only medication use)
- May miss students who seek therapy without medication

---

## Questions for Further Research

1. What specific stressors drive the 40% medication increase?
2. Why are medical/health sciences PhD students protected?
3. What interventions could mitigate the progressive deterioration?
4. How does medication use vary by discipline, advisor quality, funding status?
5. What is the long-term career impact of PhD-related mental health issues?
